## Astera Labs

His largest holding supplies connectivity for next-generation Amazon and AMD systems. WPR likes the size of the market, using Broadcom's stated $1.6 trillion value as context. That comparison is his reasoning, not a valuation target for Astera. [E03]

At the Citi conference, as WPR recounts it, management described Scorpio X entering volume production in Q3, a path to more than $1,000 of Scorpio X content per XPU, and customer engagements in the high teens versus previously 10 or more. Customers are finding different uses for the platform; management expects many engagements to become design wins ramping in 2027. WPR notices the shift toward customers directing designs. The optical roadmap starts with components in 2027 and later an optically enabled Scorpio engine. [E03]

He also reviews a September 15 LEO release claiming 62% faster time to first token and a 22% tokens-per-second improvement for LEO X, plus doubled memory bandwidth and capacity for LEO 2 E/P products. These are company claims relayed by WPR, without independent benchmark verification. [E04]

## Silicon Motion

WPR calls Silicon Motion his next high-confidence stock. His thesis centers on boot drives and enterprise expansion while memory makers retreat from controllers. Conference remarks put PC controller share at 40% and mobile share at 20-25%, with an Apple exclusion caveat; 95% of the business was mobile and PC a year earlier. [E05]

Management describes a large telecom win, negotiations for three or four additional boot-drive customers, and demand associated with DPUs, TPUs and switches. WPR identifies Nvidia and Google, while acknowledging that some customers are not explicitly named. His statement that a DPU is Nvidia-specific is his explanation, not an independently verified technical definition. [E05]

He recounts attach rates rising from 30-40% on the older Blackwell configuration to 100% on the integrated Vera Rubin configuration, alongside more switch content. The caption's BlueField generation wording is source-reported, not a checked specification. MonTitan, captioned "Mont Titan," reportedly added five customers beyond the original two, ramping in the second half. WPR expects customization to improve margins; management's stated customer ramp does not itself establish that outcome. [E06]

## Figure Technology

Figure remains a high-allocation conviction. Management's conference narrative is a large core mortgage opportunity, expanding partner adoption, and more assets moving through its marketplace. WPR finds management's stated 100-fold mortgage opportunity encouraging; this is an addressable-opportunity claim, not a revenue forecast. Management says the process saves $11,000 of a typical $12,000 cost and cuts 45 days to five. It describes more than 500 partners, most under 10% penetrated in their respective businesses. [E07]

Figure Connect reportedly reaches 65% of volume, above the original 60% IPO goal, with a new 70% target. Larger customers receive lower fee tiers, so blended fees can decline as volume rises. Management emphasizes total volume, revenue and margins. WPR reports 55% adjusted EBITDA margin against a 60% goal, acquisition and auto-market expansion, and a Sierra AI loan-officer assistant. Analyst observations on first-lien growth are separate from management statements. [E08]

The more consequential update is WPR's own expectations. He previously used RWA.xyz, then learned from a Reddit investor about Figure's investor metrics page. He estimates quarterly consumer loan volume near $5 billion, the midpoint of $4.8-5.2 billion guidance, below his earlier hope for more than $5 billion. Matched offers, lender supply and borrower demand reportedly rise roughly 50% sequentially. These indicators are distinct from completed loan volume. [E09]

He explores a possible rate explanation using AI. That explanation is unverified and does not prove why volume is near midpoint. He remains confident because the stock is down more than 62% from its high while the company reports record partner additions in the last two quarters. His next-report watchpoints are new partners and the next consumer-loan-volume guide. He discloses no trade. [E10]

## Ethos Technologies

WPR describes a technology platform taking share in life insurance, building brand awareness and entering adjacent markets such as annuities. A CEO podcast supplies claims of four consecutive years above 50% revenue growth, a single-digit market share and a business unconstrained by carrying insurance policies on its balance sheet. Management's wish to repurchase shares after the IPO is historical discussion, not a newly announced buyback. [E11]

The September 9 Goldman Sachs conference account describes a ten-minute purchase journey replacing a ten-week process, proprietary data and underwriting, agents recruiting other agents, and direct-channel growth of 130% with similar unit economics. WPR clarifies that "no balance sheet" means third-party insurers carry the policies; he cites $168 million of company cash. These are source-reported claims. [E12]

He then discusses Hunterbrook's short report and a distributor video. He questions the clip's context and says, "I'm not going to be spooked out of the investment by a questionable short report." This records his response, not an independent assessment or refutation of the report. No sale is disclosed. [E13]

## Onto Innovation

WPR explicitly describes increasing confidence in this newer holding. He points to Dragonfly G5's advanced-packaging inspection, faster sales following new products, and manufacturing changes that improve economics. Management's Citi presentation also describes fast Atlas G6 adoption, better customer visibility and multi-year expansion plans. [E14]

Conference figures put packaging growth at 80% annually and advanced nodes at 35%. The packaging mix is roughly 60% memory and 40% 2.5D logic, varying by quarter. Management says memory inspection share remains high while current share gains primarily involve 3D metrology. Smaller, denser bumps increase inspection demands; 2.5D products have attractive pricing and margins. [E15]

He recounts opportunities in silicon photonics, co-packaged optics, panel packaging and hybrid optical/X-ray metrology. Management reports winning against a leading competitor; WPR uses AI to infer that competitor was KLA. KLA's identity is not established by the quoted management account. The X-ray investment's name is garbled as "Rigu" and remains unresolved here. [E16]

Outsourced manufacturing reportedly accounts for 70% of sales. WPR credits these "extended factories" with about 250 basis points of gross-margin improvement and 500 basis points of operating-margin improvement over the last year. He could not identify the manufacturers. His suggestion that identities are intentionally hidden is speculation. [E16]

## Micron

The short recap follows the [October 1 earnings review](../2026-10-01_bgt3XffXMxM/report.md). WPR highlights pricing tailwinds, management's lack of visibility to supply catching demand, work with Nvidia on custom high-bandwidth memory, and $44 billion operating cash flow versus $25.4 billion sequentially and $5.7 billion a year earlier. He repeats core data-center growth of 1,025% annually and 56% sequentially, and management's expectation of sequential growth through the following year. All figures are source-reported. [E17]

He does not restate the October 1 medium-allocation plan or disclose a new weight. The captioned "NV HBM" name is not independently confirmed.

## VIAVI Solutions

WPR's thesis is that faster transceivers and co-packaged optics require more testing. He recounts broad optical-transceiver customer coverage, high optical-circuit-switch testing share, more content per port, and CPO revenue expected to start in the fall. A September 24 release on level 2 CMMC certification is presented as opening aerospace and defense opportunities. [E18]

| Operating margin | 24%; prior guide upper end 23.2% | 27.1% |
| Gross margin | 61% | Not stated |

WPR emphasizes that the guide steps up into a seasonally slower quarter. He cites management saying $500 million quarterly revenue could arrive sooner than expected, then makes his own prediction: VIAVI may guide to $500 million or more at its next report. That is not the issued $460 million guide. [E19]

He calls an $11.6 billion market cap and 6.5 times annualized quarterly sales reasonable, versus Lumentum's stated 24.2 times sales. He acknowledges Lumentum grows faster, while preferring VIAVI's margins and valuation. His conclusion is that increased testing intensity is not yet fully reflected in the price and next guidance could surprise. These are dated opinions, not verified valuations or recommendations.
