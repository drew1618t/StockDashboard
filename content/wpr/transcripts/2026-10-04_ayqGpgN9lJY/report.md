## Why he reallocated

**Micron:** he still thinks it could become a $3 trillion or $5 trillion company, but a smaller company might triple sooner. Other holdings will report again before Micron. These are his opportunity-cost judgments, not a deterioration claim about Micron's recent results. [E04]

**Lumentum:** the market already recognizes its optical leadership. He considers Coherent and VIAVI more compelling on relative valuation. He still likes Lumentum's business. No Coherent purchase is disclosed. [E04] [E11]

**Astera:** the position was oversized; his confidence remains very high as custom silicon grows. [E04]

**MACOM and Semtech:** reviewing their production-ready products and specialty semiconductor releases raised his confidence. **Kulicke & Soffa:** he continues to see undervaluation. [E04]

## Updates on the smaller main holdings

### FormFactor: testing complexity supports the thesis

WPR expects the company's target of doubling revenue and more than doubling adjusted earnings per share by 2030 to be reached sooner. That timing is his forecast. The captions say “adjusted DPS”; the intended metric has not been checked against the presentation. [E05]

In his account of the May investor day, management describes more testing as HBM stacks become more valuable and complex. A 1% per-layer error can compound into about a 15% defect rate for a 16-layer stack. Customized probe cards and longer test times support demand. He recounts a Texas facility expansion, scale economics and a 55% gross-margin target at the discussed levels; this is a target, not a verified current margin. [E06]

At the September 9 conference, management reportedly described a move toward advanced MEMS probes, GPU pilot production and supply to all three major DRAM manufacturers. Its CPO revenue expectation moved from $10m-$20m for 2026 to $20m by the end of Q3, as WPR recounts it. Capacity constraints and the Texas expansion matter to delivery. He explicitly does **not** know whether references to Google and Amazon establish customer relationships. [E07]

### MACOM: product breadth increased confidence

He is impressed by a long product list spanning optical and copper connectivity, amplifiers, detectors, PCIe, near-package optics, free-space optics and radio-frequency transport over fiber. These are product-release descriptions, not quantified revenue contributions. His suggestion that the European roadshow may help win European customers is an inference. The confirmed addition is the 1% disclosed earlier. [E04] [E08]

### Kulicke & Soffa: valuation versus execution risk

WPR highlights thermocompression and fluxless bonding products aimed at advanced packaging and CPO. He calls the company undervalued at a stated $5bn market cap, citing $330m quarterly revenue and guidance above 100% annual growth next quarter. Those figures are source-reported. The new CEO is a “wild card,” but he is willing to give him a chance. [E09]

### Cohu: possible upside to the next guide

With little new conference or press-release material, he revisits earnings. He cites thermal-control differentiation, HBM inspection shipments, a Taiwan OSAT qualification and CPO engagements. The caption's wording around computing orders, 46% of revenue and 150% growth is imprecise; do not treat it as a clean segment breakdown. At a stated $3.1bn valuation and $170m revenue guide, about 35% annual growth, he thinks the next guidance could surprise. That upside is his expectation. [E10]

### Lumentum: strong demand, recognized valuation

In WPR's conference recap, management describes demand exceeding supply, favorable pricing, a larger OCS opportunity at its largest customer and increasing CPO intensity. He recounts three fabrication ramps and a projected fourfold pump-laser volume ramp over five quarters. These are management statements retold by WPR. His conclusion remains positive on the business but qualified by the valuation already assigned to its growth. [E11]

### MKS: operating leverage and memory exposure

He emphasizes NAND facility upgrades, photonics, proprietary chemistry with high margins and equipment attach rates, and capacity expansion. His website review helped him understand the product range; the ESG release added little investment information. He sees profitability improving as revenue scales. This episode does not revisit or resolve the debt concern in his earlier analysis. [E12]

### Red Violet: earnings must test management's enthusiasm

He recounts a September 9 deal covering more than 10,000 California realtor members. His main question is whether management's enthusiasm signals a coming acceleration beyond the stated 23% revenue growth, or simply promotional language. He plans to stay through the next earnings report to find out. [E13]

### Semtech: a confirmed addition and a separate intention

WPR reviews seven product releases covering optical amplifiers, network chipsets, surge protection, wireless devices and a Palo Alto Networks integration. He likes the pace of products reaching production. Near the end he says he is considering adding back despite the stock's rise. The earlier 0.6% purchase is confirmed; this later remark does not prove a second purchase or establish its timing. [E04] [E14]
