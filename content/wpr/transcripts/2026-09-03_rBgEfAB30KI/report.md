# Credo: the first major reduction before the exit

September 3, 2026 · *Credo's $CRDO earnings report update* · Earnings reported September 1

[Source video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rBgEfAB30KI) · [Formatted transcript](transcript.md) · [Exact evidence](evidence.md) · [Structured company view](company_views.json)

WPR says he reduced Credo from an original roughly 10% allocation to 4% the day after its earnings report. He retained shares while reassessing. The central issue was that revenue and guidance fell short of his own expectations and other holdings offered stronger immediate growth. This was not a revenue miss against the company's guidance or the analyst estimates he cites. [E02] [E04] [E05] [E14]

This is the first stage in the three supplied September videos: a reduction to 4% disclosed September 3, a 1.7% remaining allocation disclosed September 6, and a full exit disclosed September 13. The chronology section below links each stage to its own source. September 13 remains the latest supplied opinion reference.

All financial figures and management statements here are WPR's account of the report. They have not been independently verified against company filings or audio. WPR's own hypotheses are identified separately. Later price performance is not used to judge what he knew at the time.

## Expectations versus results

WPR identifies the earnings period as fiscal Q1 2027, covering May through July 2026. He set a more demanding hurdle than the company or analysts, partly because Credo had previously guided above his expectations and Astera Labs had reported strong active-electrical-cable sales. [E02] [E13]

Revenue beat the company's ceiling by $4 million, but was below WPR's desired $490 million level. New guidance also exceeded the cited analyst expectation while falling materially below his $580 million hurdle. “Disappointed WPR” and “missed consensus” would therefore describe different events; only the former fits these revenue comparisons. [E02] [E04] [E05]

He reports new GAAP gross-margin guidance of 62.9%-64.9%, adjusted gross margin of 67%-69%, GAAP operating expense of $199m-$204m, and adjusted operating expense of $100m-$105m. The continued lower GAAP margin merits attention in his review. [E05]

## When does the growth ramp arrive?

Before earnings, WPR had seen an earlier management statement calling fiscal 2027 an inflection year for optical products. He initially expected a significant step-up at the beginning of that year. Other earlier commentary referred to an aggressive second-half ramp, and he was unsure whether every reference meant calendar 2026 or fiscal 2027. He chose not to change his position before the report. [E03]

After reviewing the release and call, his working interpretation is that the heavier ramp belongs to the second half of fiscal 2027. He recounts management reiterating that timing and more than $600 million of optical revenue for fiscal 2027, unchanged from the preceding quarter. The $600 million is presented as a fiscal-year optical estimate; it should not be relabeled as all second-half revenue. [E06] [E09]

He lays out an analyst path of roughly $530 million, $639 million, and $843 million over the following three quarters. The $639 million estimate faces a $407 million year-earlier comparison, implying about 57% annual growth. He worries the market may dislike that headline after much faster growth. The later $843 million quarter would represent a substantial ramp, but the investment question becomes whether to wait two or three quarters when other stocks may offer stronger results sooner. These are analyst forecasts and WPR's market-reaction hypothesis, not completed results. [E12]

He reports that the analyst fiscal-year estimates imply roughly 87% annual growth, broadly consistent with management's 85% outlook. He therefore sees a timing and opportunity-cost problem even though the full-year story remains promising. [E12]

## What remains attractive about Credo

WPR recounts management saying that AEC remains the largest business and continues growing, while optical is the fastest-growing area. He describes record optical-DSP revenue, first silicon-photonics revenue, production shipments of zero-flap optics, and additional hyperscaler and neocloud ramps. He also discusses near-package optics, an expanded product family, and management's argument for owning an integrated product stack. [E07] [E11]

Customer concentration improves in his account. The four largest customers represent 33%, 28%, 13%, and 10% of revenue, totaling 84%. He compares this favorably with his recollection that one customer previously accounted for more than 80%. An improvement in concentration does not mean concentration is low; the current figures still leave most sales with four customers. [E08]

He recounts acquisition-related design wins for fiscal 2028 and management expressing confidence about supplying greater second-half volumes. These are future opportunities and management assurances, not proof that the near-term quarter will beat. [E10]

## Expenses and margins: some explanation, some uncertainty

WPR notices that both gross margin and operating expenses performed worse than the GAAP guidance he reports. He does not treat this as an automatic warning sign and looks for an explanation. He cites R&D rising from $90.5 million to $114.5 million sequentially and SG&A rising from $51.7 million to $73.8 million, initially interpreting this as hiring and scaling. He later recounts the CFO explicitly attributing increased adjusted operating expense to R&D investment. [E04] [E06] [E08]

That explanation supports the operating-expense interpretation. The passages do not independently establish why GAAP gross margin fell; an R&D or SG&A increase should not be treated here as a demonstrated reconciliation of gross margin. Adjusted operating margin is reported at 48.2% versus 49.6% in the prior quarter. [E08] [E09]

He reports $764 million of cash after substantial cash use for the Dust Photonics acquisition and would like more detail about its revenue contribution. He also cites $313 million of inventory, up $62 million sequentially, which he considers likely tied to customer orders. That inventory explanation is his inference rather than a verified order-to-inventory reconciliation. [E09]

Adjusted net income is inconsistent in the captions: one passage gives $263 million and another $236 million. The latter passage also describes 4% sequential growth, while both discussions refer to strong annual growth. This report retains the conflict rather than selecting one amount for a financial model. [E06] [E09]

## Decision sequence and stated reasons

1. **Before earnings:** he makes no change, intending to evaluate the results. [E03]
2. **After the release:** he waits for the call instead of acting in the after-hours market. He says he normally acts after hours only when he sees a large price anomaly. A hypothetical trim if the stock had risen sharply is not an executed trade. [E06]
3. **The following day:** he reduces the position from roughly 10% to 4%. He says most of his sales were around $170 and $165, despite a premarket price around $191. These are his reported execution details, not verified brokerage records. [E14]
4. **Use of proceeds:** he adds to Astera Labs, Silicon Motion, Onto Innovation, Micron, and Figure Technology. He promises more allocation detail in the extended portfolio review. [E15]
5. **At the time of the video:** he remains invested and continues evaluating whether he is comfortable with the remaining allocation. [E14] [E15]

His explanation combines two disappointing quarters relative to his own expectations, a later growth ramp than hoped, and opportunity cost versus holdings that are still accelerating. He also acknowledges that Astera and Credo follow different technology roadmaps: strength in a shared product category did not imply identical timing for both companies. [E13] [E14]

He suggests that the call's emphasis on product descriptions might compensate for weaker headline numbers. That is his interpretation of management's presentation, not an established motive. He also says his strategy does not depend on always achieving excellent selling prices. [E13] [E14]

## What this adds to company comparisons

The following are analytical applications of this episode, rather than new rules attributed to WPR:

- Record the three separate hurdles: company guidance, consensus, and WPR's own target. A beat on the first two can still prompt a reduction if it misses the third. [E02] [E04] [E05]
- Date the expected catalyst precisely. “Second half” is not enough when the fiscal year differs from the calendar year. [E03] [E09]
- Compare timing across alternatives. WPR can view the annual outlook as plausible while preferring a holding with stronger immediate growth. [E12] [E14]
- Keep a peer's success as context rather than proof. WPR explicitly revisits his inference from Astera's AEC strength. [E13]
- Separate the action from the strength of the business opinion. A substantial trim, continued reassessment, and eventual exit are different stages. [E14] [E15]

For future Credo comparisons, the September 13 source governs the latest supplied holding status. This video preserves the earlier expectations and uncertainty that explain how he got there.
