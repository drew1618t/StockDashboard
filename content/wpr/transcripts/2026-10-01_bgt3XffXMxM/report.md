# Micron: a record quarter, a flat reaction, and a plan to hold

October 1, 2026 · *Micron MU earnings report review - September 30, 2026* · Earnings reported September 30

[Source video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bgt3XffXMxM) · [Formatted transcript](transcript.md) · [Exact evidence](evidence.md) · [Structured company view](company_views.json)

WPR says the fiscal Q4 2026 report confirms the memory narrative and raises his confidence that revenue will grow sequentially through 2027. His stated action is a plan to **maintain a medium allocation**. He discloses no trade and no new weight in this video. [E16]

Revenue and next-quarter revenue guidance cleared both the analyst consensus and his own higher hurdles. Earnings per share and its guide landed just below his targets but above consensus; he calls them "right about where I was looking" and "pretty close." He then asks why the stock was flat after hours, calls the valuation reasonable, and weighs the law of large numbers against Micron growing faster than his smaller holdings. [E04] [E17]

All financial figures and management statements here are WPR's account of the press release, slides, earnings call, and post-earnings call. They have not been checked against company filings or audio. Later price performance is not used to judge this view.

Before the report he noted the prior release's wording that fiscal Q4 gross margin reflected "a meaningful moderation in the rate of price increases." He expected strong headline numbers regardless and thought the market would focus on the next guide. His review of the prior transcript found no strong hints about the quarter. [E02] [E03]

Operating expenses doubled against guidance. He had flagged this in his September 30 quick-take post and resolved it from the call: management gave employees additional compensation for the company's performance. That is the explanation he reports; the cost split is not detailed. [E05] [E12]

## Cash flow and the full year

He calls operating cash flow the standout figure: $44bn versus $25.4bn the prior quarter and $5.7bn a year earlier. He attributes much of the gain to higher memory prices, describing this as what he was "able to gather" rather than a management breakdown. [E06]

Because the quarter closed the fiscal year, he also reports full-year figures:

| Full-year measure | Fiscal 2026 | Fiscal 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $133.2bn | $37.3bn |
| Gross margin | 80.7% | 39.8% |
| Operating income | $99.3bn | $9.8bn |
| Net income | $85bn | $8.5bn |

He describes net income as up 1,000%. The captioned figures are a tenfold increase, which is a 900% rise. [E06]

His standouts are the data center ramp from a small base, the 47.8% sequential auto and embedded growth, and 90% gross margins in two segments. He finds it notable that mobile and client, "probably not the most exciting part of the business," has the highest operating margin. [E09]

## What management said, as WPR recounts it

**Demand and supply.** The slides say demand strengthened since the last report and supply-demand conditions should be tighter for the next two years, with no line of sight to balance. [E10] [E11]

**Customer agreements.** Strategic customer agreements rose from 16 at the prior report to 26, covering 35% of sales through 2030. The slides say 75% of the agreements have fixed pricing and the rest are negotiated at market prices. Some now extend to 2031, and customers want agreements beyond 2030. Management also says 75% of total 2027 memory output is already committed, with 2028 under negotiation. The 75% fixed-price share of agreements and the 75% committed share of 2027 output are separate figures. [E03] [E10] [E12] [E13]

**Bit growth.** For NAND, calendar 2026 bit shipments grew in the low 20% range, above company expectations, and calendar 2027 and 2028 are expected in the mid 20s. DRAM grew in the mid 20s in 2026 and is expected in the low 20s for 2027 and 2028. WPR reads this as a flip toward faster NAND than DRAM bit growth. DRAM was 73% of revenue and NAND 26%. [E11]

**Outlook.** Management expects fiscal 2027 to be another record year with sequential growth each quarter, and fiscal Q1 2027 to be the floor for gross margin. WPR calls the sequential-growth statement very strong given current record levels. [E11]

**HBM and Nvidia.** HBM revenue grew faster than total revenue and is expected to keep outgrowing DRAM. Most calendar 2027 HBM supply is already under agreement. Micron is working with Nvidia on the first custom HBM4E, a program the post-earnings call says has run for over a year. The HBM trade ratio further constrains supply. [E10] [E12] [E13] [E14]

**Balance sheet.** Cash reached $73.5bn, up from $26bn, against $5.2bn of debt, for $68.3bn net cash. Three agencies rate the credit BBB+. Management plans to return 100% of excess cash to shareholders. [E13]

**Capex.** Next-quarter capex is projected at $11.5bn and the following quarter at $13.5bn, rising further in the second half of fiscal 2027. Domestic buildout is in Virginia, Idaho, and New York, alongside fabs in Japan, Taiwan, and Singapore. [E10] [E13]

**China.** China is expected to be a single-digit share of fiscal 2027 revenue, and management claims a lead of at least two nodes over Chinese competition. [E14]

**Products and people.** Micron is sampling a 512GB DDR5 module and LPDDR6 for physical AI markets, its server SOCAMM portfolio more than doubled sequentially, and it won every tier-1 OEM for an AI workstation product WPR wants to understand better. A new COO and CTO were appointed and led the post-earnings call. [E07] [E11] [E14]

## One pushback from the post-earnings call

An analyst noted that mobile and client bit shipments declined for a second consecutive quarter. Management confirmed it and cited higher pricing and favorable mix. WPR connects this to the segment's top operating margin and infers that Micron is being selective in mobile. That selectivity is his interpretation. Management also describes some flexibility to choose which memory to produce with similar tooling, and says negotiations reflect the higher pricing environment. [E15]

## Conclusion, action, and valuation

WPR's conclusions, in his own framing: [E16]

- The report confirms the memory narrative is on track.
- He has more confidence that revenue increases sequentially through 2027.
- The balance-sheet turnaround, buybacks, and accumulating cash are likely to improve the stock's value.
- He plans to maintain a medium allocation.

He then poses the question of whether everything is priced in, given the flat after-hours move. He calls a run-rate price-to-sales of 5.5 reasonable alongside industry-leading margins. He weighs the law of large numbers, saying it is harder to triple sales from about $50bn to $150bn, then asks whether all else is equal, since Micron is growing faster than any of his smaller companies. He says that faster growth is one of the main reasons he likes the stock. [E17]

The captions say "3 billion or 5 billion market cap." Trillion is probably intended. As a rough check, 5.5 times an annualized $54.2bn quarter is about $1.2 trillion, so tripling sales would point to a multi-trillion valuation. That arithmetic is this report's, not WPR's. The $50bn-to-$150bn sales figures are quarterly, matching the current $54.2bn quarter. [E17]
