# WPR's top seven growth stocks: November 2026

November 1, 2026 · *WPR's Top 7 Growth Stocks - November 2026*

[Source video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ucnG2ZJSnq8) · [Formatted transcript](transcript.md) · [Exact evidence](evidence.md) · [Structured company view](company_views.json)

WPR reviews his seven largest holdings ahead of earnings season. MACOM and DigitalOcean appear in his monthly top seven for the first time, replacing Micron and VIAVI from the October 4 list. He plans to hold Figure until its November 12 report and then re-evaluate, expects Ethos to raise full-year guidance again, expects MACOM to easily beat analyst estimates, and will re-evaluate Onto on its November 9 report. No purchases, sales or weights are disclosed. [E01] [E03] [E14] [E18] [E23]

Financial figures, product claims and analyst views below are WPR's account of company releases, calls and research. They have not been checked against filings or audio. Later price performance is not used to judge these views.

## Performance

His self-reported returns are 146% in 2024, 112% in 2025, 5% year to date in 2026, and "about 448%" cumulative since the start of 2024. The year-to-date path he describes: down 20% in March, up 62% at the end of June, up 22% after a July drop, up 31% at the end of September, and up 5% at the end of October. He says October "has been really hard on AI infrastructure names." [E02]

These are unaudited statements. The figures compound consistently (2.46 × 2.12 × 1.05 ≈ 5.48), and going from +31% to +5% implies roughly a 20% decline in October. That arithmetic is this report's. On October 4 he reported 583% cumulative at +31% year to date. [E02] [October 4 E02](../2026-10-04_C8HKM_gFqT4/evidence.md#E02)

He says he is "sticking with my strategy" and expects "many of my portfolio companies will outperform" in earnings season. That is an expectation, not a result. [E02] [E24]

## Ethos Technologies

His largest holding is "disrupting the legacy life insurance market with a technology platform," with a data moat that gives carriers, partners and agents confidence to manage risk. He cites direct-to-consumer growth of 131% and an expanded Liberty Mutual relationship: Ethos life insurance is sold through Liberty Mutual call centers and online, and Ethos customers can get a discount on Liberty home and auto insurance. Management describes a massive market opportunity and a data flywheel that improved unit economics. [E03]

WPR: "I'm expecting Ethos to be able to raise their full-year guidance again this quarter." Ethos reports Monday, November 2. The captions attach the earnings date to the start of the Astera section; context places it with Ethos. [E03]

The October 4 conference account gave direct-channel growth as 130%. The October 25 episode trimmed Ethos 0.8% for sizing while he stayed confident. This video gives no new sizing. [October 4 E12](../2026-10-04_C8HKM_gFqT4/evidence.md#E12) [October 25 E06](../2026-10-25_ZE47NKuIX_c/evidence.md#E06)

## Astera Labs

WPR describes "next generation AI networking hardware" growing across products. Points he relays from company releases: [E04] [E05] [E06]

- Scorpio X is hitting scale for hyperscaler ASIC programs such as Amazon's Trainium (captioned "Trainium for chip"; the generation is unresolved).
- Cosmos software lets customers design and debug their own solutions and handles fleet management and diagnostics.
- Astera says it was first to market with PCIe 7 AI solutions. Aries spans copper and optical interconnects, and AMD and Intel have validated Aries smart retimers.
- On October 12, Astera, Lumentum and Corning demonstrated PCIe over optics at rack scale, combining Scorpio switches, Lumentum VCSEL optics and Corning multimode fiber. Lumentum management said it "will unlock a whole new class of functionality."
- Orion is a set of modular reference designs for AI inference for neoclouds and enterprises: up to 160 accelerators per pod, six reference designs, Cosmos management, available Q1 2027.

He offers no concern or new plan here. Astera reports Tuesday, November 3, and he will release an open earnings video on November 4 at 4:30 p.m. Eastern. [E07] [E25]

## Silicon Motion

This is a recap of the [October 29 earnings review](../2026-10-29_5zGsnheZAkM/report.md). He repeats controller share gains, Nvidia and Google as boot-drive customers, Q3 revenue of $560m (131% annual, 24% sequential growth), and a Q4 guide of $616m at the top end (124% annual, 10% sequential). [E08] [E09]

He attributes the softer sequential guide to consumer markets, where PC and smartphone makers struggle to pass rising memory costs on to consumers. Enterprise and AI solutions now make up 40% of revenue and grew "a surprising 75 to 80%" sequentially. Gross margin, adjusted EPS and operating margin hit records. [E09]

Two reconciliations with October 29: the 40% share and 75-80% growth matched the Ferri and boot-drive segment there, here called "enterprise and AI." He gives 124% annual growth here; October 29 captions had both 121% and 124%. He does not repeat the October 29 plan to maintain a high allocation, and this silence does not withdraw it. [October 29 E06](../2026-10-29_5zGsnheZAkM/evidence.md#E06) [October 29 E08](../2026-10-29_5zGsnheZAkM/evidence.md#E08) [October 29 E15](../2026-10-29_5zGsnheZAkM/evidence.md#E15)

## MACOM Technology

**Business.** MACOM works in photonics and communications semiconductors. Data center grew 40% sequentially and is 29% of sales. Fabs in Massachusetts, North Carolina, Michigan and France are expanding, and the company is a US Department of Defense trusted foundry. Over 20 customers bring in more than $10m a year each, and the top 10 make up under 40% of revenue. [E15]

**Products.** Free-space optics optical amplifiers boost laser signals between satellites and the ground. Photodiodes and photoreceivers convert incoming light to electrical signals. End markets span industrial, aerospace and defense, data center and telecom. [E16]

**Financials, as WPR reports them.** [E17]

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $342m; +36% annually, +18% sequentially |
| Next-quarter revenue guide | $415m-$425m; +61% annually and +23% sequentially at midpoint |
| Adjusted operating income | Record $108m; +70% annually, +34% sequentially |
| Adjusted net income | +30% sequentially |
| Gross margin | 58.3%, versus 55.3% a year earlier and 56.9% last quarter |

He says MACOM "has never posted a over 50% year over year revenue growth number before," so the market may "re-evaluate the company as a higher growth stock after this report." Management says it can pass most price increases on to customers. The growth-history claim is not independently checked. [E17]

**Why he likes it.** The strongest guide ever while the stock is about $235, versus a $418 high in June. He also cites diverse customers across photonics, satellite communications, owned and expanding fabs, an R&D focus, and operating and net income growing faster than revenue. [E18]

**Analyst estimates.** After the projected $420m quarter, analysts have $431m, $441m, $453m and $467m. "I would expect MACOM to easily surpass these low growth estimates." MACOM reports Thursday, November 5, premarket. [E18]

The stock price is about 44% below the June high, and the analyst path implies about 3% average sequential growth. That arithmetic is this report's. Earlier sources show MACOM as a September 13 starter, added on September 20, October 4 and October 11, and held at 4.9% on October 25. This is his first full thesis restatement since the October 11 additions. [October 11 report](../2026-10-11_6_1nOtQ9CB4/report.md)

## DigitalOcean

DigitalOcean is a "fast-growing cloud platform for Agentech AI" (agentic AI) competing with the hyperscalers, neoclouds and startups. Once focused on startups, it is moving up market to enterprise. He cites guidance for over 50% revenue growth in 2027. Last quarter, inference engine customers grew 60% month over month and token volume rose more than 30x in 60 days. "The inference engine is off a small base, but I'm really interested to see what management says about the growth." No report date is given. [E19]

Product updates he relays: [E20] [E21]

- Agent Droplets: a single monthly subscription with a free trial, a harness runtime with a dedicated sandbox, included inference, an action gateway to more than 16,000 tools, observability and enterprise security.
- October 12: agent learning and data services with insights (records agent actions), signals (flags failures) and evaluation and simulation (tests changes before customers see them), plus an inference router optimized for cost. Its "teams on evaluations" wording is garbled.
- October 13: a Bright Data search partnership through the action gateway.

He bought DigitalOcean on October 18, choosing it over Cloudflare on long-term guidance and profitability, and added 0.2% on October 25. [October 18 report](../2026-10-18_wzsOkiMNb5E/report.md) [October 25 E03](../2026-10-25_ZE47NKuIX_c/evidence.md#E03)

## Onto Innovation

Onto's Dragonfly inspection and measurement tool "is gaining traction where it's winning heads-up against much larger legacy incumbents like KLA Corporation." Onto received SK Hynix's best partner award on October 12 and supplies testing equipment for HBM. [E22]

He cites RBC's initiation: a $400 price target, positioning for strong wafer-fab-equipment spending, advanced packaging expected to grow 80% this year, HBM and chip-on-wafer-on-substrate demand, and the Dragonfly G5 ramp. These are the analyst's claims, not WPR's. [E22]

Onto reports Monday, November 9: "I'll be reevaluating the company based on the strength of the report." He states no confidence level this time; on October 4 it ranked fifth with "increasing confidence." [E23] [October 4 E14](../2026-10-04_C8HKM_gFqT4/evidence.md#E14)

## Final thoughts and strategy

"It was a challenging month for many of my stocks but I'm sticking to my process of investing behind what I consider to be the best growth stock names and I'm reevaluating often based on new information such as earnings." [E24]

Three strategy observations, labeled as this report's reading:

- Process over drawdown: he frames a 20-point October decline as part of his strategy's swings rather than a reason to change approach. [E02] [E24]
- Weekly data versus reports: for Figure he avoids concluding from seasonal weekly data and defers judgment to earnings. [E14]
- Estimate gaps: for MACOM he pairs a record guide and a stock well below its high with analyst estimates he considers too low. He states this for MACOM only. [E17] [E18]
