## Forgent Power Solutions: executed starter

Forgent sells electrical distribution equipment and factory-built integrated powertrain systems for data centers, utilities and industrial facilities. WPR likes the move toward customized systems, where engineering involvement and a complete solution can raise margins. The caption repeatedly garbles its name. The [company's investor site](https://ir.forgentpower.com/) confirms Forgent Power Solutions and ticker FPS; this identity check does not independently validate the financial analysis. [E05]

He reports quarterly revenue of $462m, up 94% annually and 22% sequentially, against $432m implied guidance. Fiscal-year revenue guidance of $2.4bn-$2.6bn implies 76% growth at the midpoint. He cites a $12.3bn market cap, run-rate P/S of 6.7, $98m cash and $704m debt. All are his source-reported figures. The next-quarter revenue guide is roughly flat sequentially, which he attributes to seasonality. [E05]

In his earnings-call retelling, management says quarterly bookings exceeded $1.5bn, book-to-bill reached 3.3 times, and backlog reached $3bn. It describes firm purchase orders rather than letters of intent and says backlog covers more than 90% of revenue guidance. WPR says a first frontier AI-lab order and a hyperscaler agreement helped push him into buying. The customers are unnamed in this source. [E06] [E07] [E08]

Management's reported data-center revenue growth of 161% substantially exceeds customer-count growth of 18%; average customer revenue reportedly rose 121%. WPR sees larger, more integrated engagements as part of the opportunity. Capacity expansion, engineering investment and continued hiring support the ramp, but also create execution demands. [E07] [E08]

His position remains small because growth depends on physical labor and onboarding many employees, while private-equity sponsor Neos Partners may sell more shares. He calls sponsor ownership a permanent yellow flag and dislikes the CFO's limited call participation. His claimed 37% sponsor voting stake and prior offerings are source-reported. Information from AI tools about future offerings is not a company commitment or proof that selling has finished. [E05] [E07] [E09]

He had watched Forgent after its first two quarters and wanted evidence that acceleration would persist. New fiscal guidance, profitability growing faster than revenue and new AI customers supplied that evidence. He describes the energy exposure as small diversification after selling Bloom Energy, while acknowledging Forgent remains heavily tied to AI infrastructure. The opening disclosure is 1.1%; the later conclusion calls it a 1% position. Retain these rounded descriptions separately. [E09]

## Penguin Solutions: reconsidered, without a purchase

WPR briefly held Penguin in August and recounts selling amid CEO/CFO uncertainty and less attractive margins than other opportunities. Now he sees an apparent inflection: new AI infrastructure customers, a fuller AI-factory offering and a newly appointed CFO. His statement that the new CEO is doing well is an interpretation of business results lining up with the appointment, not established causation. [E13] [E15]

He reports $567m quarterly revenue, up 68% annually and 18% sequentially, against a $3.7bn market cap and run-rate P/S of 1.6. In his management recap, integrated memory contributes $341m, 60% of revenue, and grows 158% annually. Advanced computing is much slower at $154m and 11% annual growth, while legacy LED revenue is $72m and grows 7%. The source's aggregate AI-business growth claim does not establish that neocloud contracts already drive most revenue. [E13] [E14] [E15]

He expects newer neocloud contracts to contribute more to advanced computing, which is reportedly guided for 40% full-year growth. His explanation that two Penguin Edge customers did not renew comes from AI tools and remains unverified. He also notes a gross-margin discrepancy between his data provider and the call. [E14] [E15]

The conclusion is to consider a starter, possibly funded by another holding that disappoints during earnings season. This is a conditional future reallocation, not an executed trade or a current holding disclosure. Leadership turnover still concerns him. [E16]

## Passed ideas

### Chime Financial

WPR likes newer products, enterprise workplace adoption and instant-loan originations growing 70% sequentially. He reports $670m quarterly revenue, 27% annual growth, and run-rate P/S around 4. Management's member-growth and consumer-health comments are retold claims. [E10] [E11]

He wants higher growth before reconsidering. SoFi's faster source-reported growth gives him a comparison, and a planned CFO retirement leaving an interim successor does not inspire confidence. His suggestion that there may be more to the departure is speculation. Questions about sequentially lower profitability remain unresolved. [E10] [E12]

### CareDx

The captions call CareDx “KDX,” but give ticker CDNA and describe transplant monitoring. WPR reports $132m revenue, up 52% annually, yet says the full-year guide implies only $125m average revenue across the remaining two quarters. A lab sale boosted stated GAAP income; he says income would otherwise have been negative. [E17]

He questions how testing-volume growth and lower assumed revenue per test fit the optimistic commentary. He wants to see growth and profitability develop before investing. The report does not turn the lab-sale arithmetic into independently verified recurring earnings. [E17]

### Silicom

WPR describes Israeli networking hardware, server cards and edge systems, with $23.8m quarterly revenue, 56% annual growth and 25% sequential growth. He finds the valuation reasonable or potentially cheap and sees interest in a profitability crossover. The earlier $45m quarterly revenue peak in 2022 gives him pause. This is a passed recovery idea, not a new holding. The caption's “SIC” ticker is garbled; [Silicom's own release](https://www.silicom-usa.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/PRQ222.pdf) confirms SILC. This is an identity check only. [E18]

## Figure: improved preliminary volume after the trim

The company release, as recounted by WPR, puts preliminary consumer loan volume at $5.12bn within the prior $4.8bn-$5.2bn guide. This is better than the roughly $5bn he expected from the dashboard. He says the acquisition captioned “Kyavi” was outside the volume guide and contributes one month of Q3 revenue. His [October 7 forum post, number 6](https://discussion.fool.com/t/126667/6), resolves the name as Kiavi through a quoted company release, which specifically describes one month of loan volumes included in Q3 financial results. The exact revenue contribution is not supplied by this video. [E19]

He reports Democratized Prime matched offer balances of $588m, borrower demand of $600m and lender supply of $825m, growing 50%, 45% and 58% sequentially. These are reported platform metrics, not revenue or realized earnings. He plans to retain a high allocation and watch the next report. The preliminary release does not establish full-quarter financial results. [E19]
