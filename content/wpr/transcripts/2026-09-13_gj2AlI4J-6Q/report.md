## MACOM: buying the next quarter's acceleration

WPR's interest rests more on the next quarter than on the latest result. He reports $342 million of revenue, up 36% year over year and 18% sequentially, versus a $331 million to $339 million guide. The next guide is $415 million to $425 million, which he describes as about 61% annual and 23% sequential growth. He thinks a result above that guidance could impress the market. He quotes a $20.5 billion market cap and 15× run-rate sales. [E08]

He also sees a different price/growth combination than several months earlier: roughly $280 per share versus a May peak of $418, while reported growth and the outlook have improved. His conclusion is that valuation has become more reasonable, although it remains “pricey a little bit.” He opens a starter position while continuing to learn the business. This is an expressed expectation, not evidence that the next quarter will beat. [E08] [E13]

The business breadth supports the thesis. He describes industrial and defense as 45% of sales, data center as 29%, and telecom as 26%, alongside four owned fabs. In his earnings-call review, data-center revenue grows 40% sequentially, the customer base broadens, and annual data-center growth is projected at 74%, following 48% and 35% in the preceding two years. New optical products and satellite programs add potential growth sources. These are management and financial claims as recounted by WPR. [E07] [E09] [E12]

He reports adjusted gross margin of 59.7%, adjusted operating income of $108 million, and operating cash flow of $80 million, with more than $100 million of operating cash flow expected next quarter. He is not concerned about rising inventory because he thinks it likely supports near-term sales. That inventory explanation is WPR's inference. [E11]

For future comparisons, MACOM is his example of paying a relatively high sales multiple for an anticipated growth inflection. It is also direct evidence that he will buy before fully understanding every technical product: “my investing strategy does not require complete technological understanding to invest and I'll learn more as I go.” This does not establish that he ignores technical risk or always accepts incomplete understanding. [E10] [E13]

## VIAVI: testing demand plus margin improvement

WPR describes VIAVI as infrastructure testing and monitoring exposure. Its network and service enablement business supplies 80% of revenue and grows 69% annually in his account. He highlights optical testing, the move toward more complex transceivers and co-packaged optics, and a relationship with Lumentum. The smaller optical security and performance business supplies the other 20%. [E14]

He reports $443 million of quarterly revenue, up 53% annually and 9% sequentially, above the $437 million top end of guidance. Next-quarter guidance of $450 million to $460 million initially looked modest sequentially. Learning that the next quarter is normally the seasonal low changed his interpretation: growth through that trough increased his confidence. He cites a $9.6 billion market cap, 5.4× run-rate sales, 61% gross margin, $650 million cash, and $699 million debt. He wants better net profitability than the $33 million he reports for the quarter. [E15]

The operating-margin trajectory is a major attraction. He recounts 24% operating margin, up 280 basis points sequentially and 960 annually, followed by guidance of 27.1%. He reports a $575 million equity offering to repay a term loan. The source does not consistently label every financial metric's accounting basis; compare like-for-like company filings before using these numbers in a valuation model. [E16] [E17]

WPR reports that 800G is the main current testing driver, with 1.6T adoption building demand. Management's expected CPO revenue acceleration into December strengthens his outlook. His own expectation is that the next revenue guide can exceed $500 million. That is distinct from the company's existing $450 million to $460 million guide and its vaguer statement that a $500 million quarter may come sooner than previously expected. His suggestion that management is sandbagging and his identification of an unnamed OCS customer as Google are also interpretations, not confirmed disclosures. [E15] [E17] [E18]

He did additional research before buying: after the earnings call, he reviewed a technology-conference presentation to resolve his hesitation. The conference discussion covered engagement with the five largest transceiver manufacturers, more demanding tests for higher speeds, and new applications. He also notices tension between management describing the acquired Ethernet business as performing well and an analyst citing declining acquired-product revenue. [E18] [E19]

His final view is that the stock is priced like a slower-growth business despite improving growth and margins. Relative to MACOM, the attraction is a much lower stated sales multiple plus operating improvement. That relative framing is this report's synthesis; he does not declare VIAVI his higher-conviction choice. Both are starters. [E02] [E13] [E20]

## Everpure: interested, with unresolved growth-quality questions

WPR says a respected investor on Reddit's growth-stock-investing community brought the results to his attention. He reports $1.19 billion of revenue, 38% annual and 13% sequential growth, and an $85 million beat above the prior guidance ceiling. The next guide is $1.325 billion to $1.335 billion, while the full-year guidance ceiling rises from $4.51 billion to $5.07 billion. At roughly $33.2 billion of market value and 7× run-rate sales, he considers valuation reasonable despite the company's size. [E21]

The second top-five hyperscaler is a substantial positive in his view. His recounting of management says material revenue arrives in fiscal 2028, with minimal contribution in fiscal 2027. He translates that into an opportunity beginning roughly two quarters ahead; retain the fiscal-year statement when dating the catalyst. He also cites 54% product growth, 121% growth in storage-as-a-service total contract value, 20% ARR growth, and 44% RPO growth. These are different measures and should not be treated as interchangeable revenue growth rates. [E22]

His questions concern the composition of growth. International revenue grows 75%, versus 19% in the US; he wonders whether competitors are taking US business. He cites negative operating cash flow of $136 million and free cash flow of $238 million, declining system units, and gross margin at 68%, near the bottom of its recent range. Reinvestment and expansion are his possible explanations for the cash-flow and margin issues. His competitive explanation is a question, not an established fact. [E23]

He weighs Everpure against Micron and SanDisk: it is smaller, but he regards its growth and profitability as lower. He thinks a strong next result might exceed 45% annual growth, yet he does not announce a purchase. His actual action is to put it on his earnings calendar. [E24]

## Applied Aerospace and Defense: promising business, insufficient case to buy

WPR reports $167 million of revenue, up 47%, but only 21% organic growth after excluding an acquisition. Precision strike and space/launch show faster growth than defense/aviation. He sees $1.1 billion of backlog as encouraging relative to a stated $2.2 billion market cap, and cites $670 million to $690 million of fiscal revenue guidance. [E25] [E26]

The objections are specific: 22% gross margin, only $18 million of cash against $432 million debt, acquisition-assisted growth, uncertain effects of congressional action, and a need to see profitability after IPO expenses. He finds the lower post-IPO price attractive, but passes for now and wants to watch later results. This is evidence that low valuation and a promising end market did not overcome weak economics and uncertainty in this case. [E25] [E27]

The caption's “net income of 154 million” immediately followed by discussion of IPO expenses is ambiguous. It is deliberately excluded from the comparison figures rather than silently changed to a loss or a different number. The ticker is also inconsistent in the captions. Check the audio or original slide before using either. [E25]

## Yesway: low sales multiple does not settle the investment case

WPR reports $855 million of quarterly revenue, up 39%, a $1.6 billion market cap, and approximately 0.46× run-rate sales. He emphasizes that this is a low-margin fuel and convenience-store business, with roughly 20% gross margin and better margins inside the store than at the pump. [E28]

His concerns include Brookwood's 74% control, $618 million of debt, some growth tied to higher fuel prices, and expansion that has depended on acquisitions while new-store construction is still developing. He explicitly says control is not a complete blocker because the business began with Brookwood rather than fitting his usual concern about private-equity ownership. [E29]

He notices possible conservatism in guidance: a 40-cent-per-gallon margin assumption versus 53 cents in the reported quarter. He still passes. This episode does not support a general rule that he rejects controlled companies, all retail, or all low-margin businesses. It establishes his decision on this company with this combination of concerns. [E29]

## What to carry into company comparisons

The following is an analytical synthesis of this episode, not a new set of rules attributed verbatim to WPR.

| Comparison question | Evidence in this video | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Where is growth going next? | MACOM's next-quarter acceleration; Credo's difficult comparison; VIAVI's seasonal context. [E04] [E08] [E15] | Compare the next few reported and guided periods, including seasonality and base effects. |
| What creates the growth? | Acquisitions at Applied Aerospace; prices versus unit volumes at Everpure; fuel prices at Yesway. [E23] [E25] [E29] | Separate organic demand, pricing, mix, and acquired revenue before ranking headline rates. |
| What price is being paid for those economics? | Credo and MACOM both at a stated 15× sales; VIAVI at 5.4×; Yesway at 0.46×. [E04] [E15] [E28] | Compare margins, growth, and risk alongside the sales multiple. The smallest multiple is not automatically the best opportunity. |
| Are profits improving with revenue? | VIAVI's margin expansion versus Applied Aerospace's low gross margin and Everpure's cash-flow concerns. [E16] [E17] [E23] [E27] | Check operating leverage, cash generation, balance-sheet demands, and accounting comparability. |
| What must be sold to fund it? | Explicit Credo/SiTime exits and SanDisk trim to fund MACOM/VIAVI. [E03] [E06] | Compare a candidate with existing holdings, not only with an absolute hurdle. |
| What did he actually do? | Starters versus earnings-calendar watch versus passes. [E13] [E20] [E24] [E27] [E29] | Keep action, conviction, and interest separate. An attractive description is not a buy. |
| How does he close knowledge gaps? | MACOM learning while invested; VIAVI conference follow-up before entry. [E10] [E19] | Record what he understands, what remains uncertain, and what research changed the decision. |
