# WPR exits FormFactor and Lumentum, builds DigitalOcean, and trims Cohu

November 1, 2026 · *WPR's Full Growth Stock Portfolio Review - November 2026* · membership video

[Source video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fyBoDq2m2xc) · [Formatted transcript](transcript.md) · [Exact evidence](evidence.md) · [Structured company view](company_views.json)

WPR sold FormFactor after its production-capacity-limited guide and sold Lumentum into a reported takeover price bump. He moved most of the proceeds into DigitalOcean, with smaller additions to MACOM and MKS. He also trimmed Ethos into Figure, trimmed about a quarter of Cohu into DigitalOcean and Clear Secure, and used foreign-account cash for Phison. His strategy theme for the week is backing companies that can scale production with demand. [E03] [E06] [E07] [E08] [E09]

This is the same-day membership companion to the public [top seven video](../2026-11-01_ucnG2ZJSnq8/report.md). Financial figures and management statements are WPR's account of releases and calls, not checked against filings or audio. Later price performance is not used to judge these decisions.

## Strategy: re-evaluate, and back companies that can scale

He calls the week "another crazy week for AI related stocks with the market in freef fall on Friday." He keeps backing strong fundamentals and will look for strong guidance and visibility into 2027. [E02]

He says lower confidence after a portfolio crash is normal, and that worry can turn into numbness. "One of the most important skills in a chaotic market is the ability to re-evaluate." His test for being in the right stocks: an exceptional report, strong stated prospects, accelerating financials and a compelling narrative. He is working on a separate investor-psychology video. [E02]

The new theme is production. Many AI infrastructure companies face incredible demand, but he wants companies "which can scale up production alongside demand." FormFactor hit capacity constraints, and he notes Lumentum says it is sold out through 2029. He will "invest behind companies which prove they can scale with demand and avoid companies with just promises or ones which encounter blockers along the way." [E03]

The same lens appears later: Nanya and Macronix are capacity-limited until mid-2027, and SK hynix stands out for projecting bit growth from a new process. [E31] [E32] [E33]

Sources: [E10]. Captions give "37" and "10 cents"; cents are read from context.

He admits he set the $190m hurdle without checking seasonality. Every past Q3-to-Q4 move was down, so a $178m guide "does overcome the seasonality," which encouraged him. [E10]

Positives he lists: bookings up 126% annually and systems bookings up 318%, an AI compute pipeline of $1bn, a full-year HPC estimate raised to $120m, a goal of about three times as many Eclipse handlers in 2027, and a broad customer list. The CEO's 2027 growth projection of 30-40% "was definitely one of the factors that kept me in the stock." He guesses it will be raised. At 40%, he computes $875m of 2027 revenue against analysts' prior $770m. [E10] [E11] [E12]

From the CFO: 2027 gross margin of 46-48% and flat quarterly operating expenses of $54-56m, which he calls "a fixed cost business model." He was unsure why Q4 gross margin is guided down to 44%. In Q&A, management aims to triple Eclipse capacity and says specialty-component tightness is not a constraint today. His AI tooling guessed that the incumbent Diamond X is displacing is Teradyne; management did not name it. [E13] [E14]

Conclusion: "The headline revenue number and guidance was not that impressive, but the bookings growth and the narrative from the conference call gave me some confidence in keeping a position." He waited because he had sold FormFactor and Lumentum the same day, then trimmed on Friday. He cites a run-rate price-to-sales ratio of 3.5 on a $2.4bn market cap. [E15]

| Measure | Guide | Analysts | WPR wanted | Result or new guide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 revenue | $270m | $271m | At least $285m | $280.5m; +38% annually, +9% sequentially |
| Q4 revenue guide | n/a | $278m | Over $310m | $285m |
| Q3 gross margin | 52% | n/a | n/a | 54.7%; Q4 guided to 52.3% |
| Q3 GAAP EPS | n/a | 76 (cents) | n/a | 93 (cents) |
| Q3 adjusted EPS | n/a | 86 (cents) | n/a | Captioned "$14" |

Source: [E16]. The adjusted EPS result is likely a dropped decimal; it is left unresolved.

The light guide is explained by the existing footprint "approaching its capacity limits." He had expected the Farmers Branch, Texas expansion to ramp in time. He was surprised that the new site has already shipped a first production unit without a stronger guide. [E16] [E17]

Three things he found unconvincing:

- 2026 capex cut to $100-110m from $140-170m while production is blocked. The CFO calls it timing; WPR "did find this to be a curious drop in capex." [E18]
- Farmers Branch adds $400m of annual capacity exiting 2027. With the current footprint at a $285m quarterly guide, he computes revenue capped near $385m a quarter at the end of 2027, which "wouldn't give the company that strong of a growth rate." [E18]
- Management's "squeezed the orange" analogy "did not inspire a lot of confidence," because the constraints seemed visible quarters ago. [E19]

Positives remain: profitability heading the right way, Triton co-packaged-optics systems up 23% sequentially, and partnerships with Teradyne (a $300m investment) and Advantest. His verdict: "the guidance is too weak from my perspective to continue holding for now." He is open to re-evaluating once the factory scales and capacity proves it can keep up with demand. [E18] [E20]

FormFactor had been trimmed earlier to fund MACOM on [October 11](../2026-10-11_6_1nOtQ9CB4/report.md) and was 3.6% on October 25.

## Other holdings

**VIAVI.** Recent releases cover a Calnex network-emulation partnership, a US Army electromagnetic-spectrum selection, and an inertial navigation product using Nvidia's Jetson. He notes they are all about security, which he did not see as the main business, "but maybe it presents some upside." Reports November 4. [E21] [E35]

**Kulicke & Soffa.** Thermocompression bonding is gaining traction while "the market still prices the stock like a low growth name." There is a new CEO and a SEMI award for fluxless TCB. He wonders whether the customer that despecced at BESI switched to K&S and hopes it is not general bonding weakness. [E22]

**Red Violet.** Identity solutions disrupting incumbents with a fixed-cost model; new Forewarn community alerts. November 4 is his first earnings season holding it. [E23]

**MKS.** Guiding to record revenue of $1.35bn, up 37%, at about a $15bn market cap. It sold part of an optics business to TTM Technologies for $650m to focus and reduce debt. He thinks investor communication is unusually quiet and wants "standout financials" on November 4, since other semiconductor holdings may have stronger narratives. He plans to investigate TTM's latest report; it is not a holding. [E24]

**Clear Secure.** Added 0.3% this week, but he will re-evaluate on November 4: "I really want to see with this earnings report that the stock belongs in my portfolio." The VA pilot's expansion depends on results and carries no disclosed value. He questions why only 8.33m of 43.5m members are active. [E09] [E25]

**Semtech.** Investor day set a fiscal 2030 target of $3.2bn revenue, $2.1bn from data center, with data center growth above 100% in fiscal 2027 and 2028. "I'm wondering if the overall target is low." Gross margin floor of 64% after divesting wireless modules; quarterly guide $417.5m, up 56%. [E26]

**Penguin.** A new neocloud customer, captioned "Axe Compute" (AGPU), is small, with $3m of quarterly revenue but $3.45bn of contracted projects. He is "surprised that Penguin is getting this many Neoclouds on board this fast." [E27]

**Bloom Energy (tracker).** Revenue and profit well ahead of estimates, but the market focused on cash flow. He finds it surprising that no fuel-cell competitor has reached scale. [E28]

**Cloudflare (tracker).** Developers exceed 10m versus 7.4m on the last call. The CFO says developer count matters more than pricing; WPR thinks the company underprices its products and hurts profitability, "part of the reason I'm not making Cloudflare a full position yet." [E29]

Ethos, Astera, Silicon Motion, MACOM and Onto get no standalone section beyond allocation and trades. Their latest detailed views remain in the public [top seven report](../2026-11-01_ucnG2ZJSnq8/report.md) and, for Silicon Motion, the [October 29 review](../2026-10-29_5zGsnheZAkM/report.md).

**Macronix.** Revenue NT$24.2bn missed analysts' NT$25bn but grew 195% annually; operating margin rose to 53.4%. NOR unit shipments fell sequentially, and growth is mostly price. He finds the $6bn market cap attractive but "decided to hold off from adding," with new capacity arriving mid-2027. The planned BESI-funded addition from October 25 did not happen. [E07] [E32]

**SK hynix.** Revenue KRW104.6tn, up 328%, with an 80% operating margin. Management projects Q4 DRAM bit growth in the mid 20s from its 1c process, and long-term agreements cover 50% of 2027-2028 supply. He was surprised by the bit-growth outlook and is "debating" SK hynix against the smaller Macronix or Nanya. [E33]

**Smartoptics.** Record revenue of $34.3m, up 80% annually and 18% sequentially, and record net income. Management expects at least 25% growth in 2027, which he thinks will likely be raised. A Q2 equipment move may have flattered Q3 sequential growth. The captioned "7.6 billion USD" operating cash flow is implausible against this revenue; millions is likely. [E34]
