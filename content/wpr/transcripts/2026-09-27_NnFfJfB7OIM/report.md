## Two promising ideas, neither bought

### Avnet, AVT: distribution growth versus thin margins

Avnet is the electronics distributor that the captions repeatedly render “ANET,” “ABNET,” or “Avet,” while giving ticker **AVT**. WPR describes a global components division and smaller Farnell design/prototyping business. He reports no customer above 10% of sales. These business details come from his account, not independent filings. [E05]

He reports $8.3 billion of latest-quarter revenue, up 48% annually and 17% sequentially, ahead of a $7.3 billion to $7.6 billion guide. The next revenue guide is $9 billion to $9.3 billion, which he describes as 58% annual and 12% sequential growth at its high end. He cites $342 million EBITDA versus $163 million a year earlier, $127 million net income versus $6 million, and 10.4% gross margin. That gross margin has stayed in a narrow 10.4% to 10.6% band. He also cites $3.48 billion debt, while explaining that inventory purchases and receivables make the balance sheet more complex than a simple debt comparison suggests. [E06]

His cited $8.2 billion market cap implies roughly 0.25 times run-rate sales by his calculation. He notes a 9.5 forward PE but says he normally puts little emphasis on that estimate. From the earnings call, he recounts stronger bookings and backlog, inventory days falling to 71, regional growth, and adjusted operating margin of 3.8%. He highlights adjusted EPS guidance of $2.80 to $2.90 versus $2.28 just reported. WPR interprets management's guidance as possibly conservative because it excludes meaningful price effects. That interpretation is **his**, while the guidance itself is his account of management. [E07] [E08] [E09] [E10]

**Decision:** he added Avnet to his earnings watch list and is still considering a position. He wants confirmation that gross margin can move above its narrow range. No purchase is disclosed. His suggestion that price increases benefit Avnet is tentative, not an established operating result. [E10] [E11]

### Technoprobe, TPRO.MI: stronger margins at a higher price

WPR describes Italian probe-card maker Technoprobe as an AI and logic testing specialist and compares it with his holding FormFactor, which he sees as more memory oriented. He reports Teradyne and Advantest as strategic ecosystem partners and shareholders, not end customers. The captions garble both partner names in places, so verify exact stake figures before reuse. [E12]

Technoprobe reports in euros and by half-years, which complicates comparison with a data provider that divides six-month results into average quarters in US dollars. WPR separately cites a latest quarterly revenue of **€277 million**, up 48% sequentially, and Q3 guidance of **€314 million**. He cites six-month gross margin of 54% versus 42% in the preceding six months, with Q3 guidance of 61.5%. He reports a $23 billion market cap and 18.3 times run-rate sales. These currency units and periods should remain distinct; the source's auto captions render euro symbols poorly in some cues. [E13]

In WPR's earnings-call account, management says AI demand is the main growth driver and expects production capacity to rise 140% this year. Q3 EBITDA margin is guided to 52%. Management discusses a softer late-year GPU period offset by CPU and ASIC volume, early HBM qualification with volume expected in 2027, and a new MEMS product. WPR explicitly says he is unsure why GPU-related sales would soften. These are forward company statements as retold by WPR, not verified results. [E14] [E15]

**Decision:** WPR likes the revenue and profit inflection, high projected margins, strategic partners and European production exposure. He remains undecided about paying 18.3 times sales, particularly against owned FormFactor at a lower stated market value. He says he is debating a purchase; the closing states there were no new additions. His explanation for Technoprobe's premium on the Milan exchange is a theory, not a demonstrated cause. [E16] [E25]
