# WPR concentrates in Astera, starts AppLovin, and exits five names in earnings week

November 8, 2026 · *Growth Stock Earnings Deep Dive - 9 Portfolio Reviewed In Depth* · membership video

[Source video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Go2UN72-22Y) · [Formatted transcript](transcript.md) · [Exact evidence](evidence.md) · [Structured company view](company_views.json)

WPR reallocated repeatedly through a volatile earnings week. Astera Labs became his top position after 6.4% of stated additions. He sold MACOM after its call, sold Red Violet on weak revenue growth, sold Semtech, Cohu and Bloom Energy during Thursday's crash, and started AppLovin at 3% on a comeback report. Ethos and Clear Secure were trimmed for size. Holdings fell from 17 to 13. [E03] [E05] [E10] [E11] [E12]

He also bought Avnet on a press release and sold it again the same week when the market fell, because "I haven't read the report yet." He says the falling market "may have had some impact on how I was thinking here." [E06]

The video reviews nine Q3 reports: Ethos, DigitalOcean, Clear Secure, MKS, VIAVI, Red Violet, MACOM, Cloudflare and AppLovin. Financial figures and management statements are WPR's account of releases and calls, not checked against filings or audio. Later price performance is not used to judge these decisions.

## Strategy: process through the volatility

He describes "crazy volatility in AI related technology stocks" and "big swings in my own confidence", but says he is "continuing with my process through the volatility." He used earnings and the downturn to reallocate, and is "working towards building a more diversified portfolio gradually." Stocks that keep posting strong results "will eventually get rewarded." [E02]

His stated reasons across the trades:

- Adjust when results differ from expectations, "especially on the topline revenue number". Adding before earnings "is a double-edged sword." [E05]
- On Thursday, "one of the worst days ever for my portfolio," he reallocated "to top names where my confidence is highest." [E10]
- Keep sizes in line with confidence: Ethos over 18% and Clear Secure over 8% were both cut. [E06] [E12]
- Diversify away from being "allin on AI": Figure, Clear Secure and Red Violet were the non-AI candidates, and Clear Secure "seems to trade counter to the AI related names." [E06] [E08]
- Re-entering AppLovin: "the business prospects are way different now from last quarter." [E42]

The principles above condense his reasons for specific trades. Treating them as general rules is this report's reading.

## Ethos: top-line blowout, profitability questions

Q3 revenue was $235m, up 151%, beating guidance by 43% and his own $190m hurdle. Q4 was guided to $220-224m, above his $210m hurdle; the full year rose to $838-842m. Adjusted EBITDA of $34.9m cleared his $33m bar, but the $30-32m Q4 guide was below his $38m. GAAP net income was $4.7m, or 7 cents, against analysts' 10 cents. [E13] [E14]

From the slides he flags contribution margin at 28% (39% last year), adjusted EBITDA margin at 15% (20%) and net margin at 2% (17%). Sales and marketing rose to $169m from $57m. "Some of these profitability metrics did not land as strong as I wanted." [E15]

Management attributes the lower revenue per policy to whole-life mix and the contribution margin to the third-party shift. His seasonality view (Q1 strongest, Q2 and Q3 weaker) came from AI tooling, not management. He finds growth in two seasonally weak quarters encouraging. [E16] [E17]

His conclusion: "I would like to see more focus on profitability while the company scales up." He trimmed 5% from an oversized position, added 0.6% back on Thursday, and remains "impressed with the expansion of the platform that's disrupting a legacy industry." [E18] [E10]

## DigitalOcean: added, and surprised by the sell-off

Revenue was $321m, up 40%, against a $304-307m guide and his $325m hurdle. The Q4 guide of $348-351m was above analysts' $333m but below his $355m. The standout was guidance for over 60% revenue growth in 2027, which management says sits on a raised 2026 base and was originally 30%. [E19] [E21]

He notes AI ARR of $376m, up 288%, a record $160m ARR added, and tokens growing about 10 times sequentially. The $43m GAAP loss included a $57m convertible repurchase; RPO fell to $800m from $894m, which AI tooling suggested may reflect on-demand use. [E20]

"I found this to be an impressive report, and I was surprised by the market sell-off." He likes the $8.5bn valuation "a lot", watches competition with Cloudflare, and expects margins to recover with scale. He added 2.2% during the week. [E22] [E05] [E12]

## Clear Secure: opinion "keeps going back and forth"

Revenue of $293m grew 28% but only 5% sequentially; the Q4 guide implies 3% sequential growth. Free cash flow guidance rose to $480-490m. Members grew 31% while active members grew 15%. He was "surprised the market rallied behind this report so strongly." [E23]

Management narrative: identity "has left the airport", CrowdStrike integration into Falcon, an expanded CMS contract, 10% of US health systems, and a VA pilot "off to a strong start." [E24]

"My opinion on the stock keeps going back and forth and you can see that in the trades I made this week." Profitability stands out, valuation seems reasonable, and it trades counter to AI names, but revenue growth is not the most impressive. He added 5.6% across five steps, then trimmed 4.9% when price moves made it over 8%. This answers the November 1 plan to re-evaluate it on earnings. [E25] [E12]

## MKS: undervalued, with capacity caveats

Revenue of $1.41bn beat the $1.35bn guide, up 43%; adjusted EBITDA of $434m beat $395m. The Q4 guide is $1.49bn. [E26]

Management said it has asked thousands of suppliers to double capacity again and "there is obviously a constraint." Those comments "did have me a little concerned about their capacity to expand," though he likes the openness. [E28]

"I view MKS Inc. as a dramatically undervalued business": a $13bn market cap against $1.5bn of quarterly revenue, where he would expect about $30bn or more. He nonetheless trimmed 2.3% as "a bit more overallocated" than he wanted, leaving 2.7%. [E29] [E11]

## VIAVI: kept, smaller

Revenue of $469m, up 56%, beat the guide; Q4 is guided to $505-515m. GAAP net income of $76m against $32.7m last quarter was "one of the most impressive metrics." Network and service enablement grew 74%, 50% organic per his own lookup. [E30] [E31]

He calls a $7bn market cap cheap but cites a tough comp and acquisition-based growth: "I decided to keep an allocation in Vyavi, but with an overall smaller allocation." [E32]

## Red Violet: sold on a narrative gap

Revenue of $27.8m grew 20% and 4% sequentially, which "does not line up with management's story of signing big clients in Q2." Customer adds fell from Q2. "There just seem to be some disconnect from what I'm reading and the actual numbers they're posting." [E33] [E34]

He sold the position in two steps but added it to his Q4 earnings calendar and "would be open to reconsidering the company if they can grow revenue above 30% year-over-year." This was his first earnings season holding it. [E34] [E08] [E11]

## MACOM: sold a week after adding

"I ended up selling Maccom one day after their earnings. The financials were just okay, but the commentary on the call gave me concern." The Q4 guide implies 8% sequential growth; management expects single-digit industrial and defense growth and flat or down telecom. [E35] [E36]

Asked about Astera's transimpedance amplifier, management said product lines are very competitive and MACOM is not involved in fully integrated solutions. "I can't see owning MacOM over Astera right now." He concludes "I misunderstood the business strength of Maccom." On November 1 he had added 1% citing increasing confidence. [E36]

## Cloudflare: from tracker to position

Revenue of $782m grew 39%, and the Q4 guide of $845-846m beat analysts' $798m. He "thought I might sell off my tiny Cloudflare position" on a $34.1m GAAP net loss, until the CFO said GAAP profitability is expected in Q4, well ahead of the end-2028 investor-day milestone. [E37]

Management cited 158,000 paying customers added, up from a record 80,000 last quarter, and over 10 million active developers. He still views the stock as "expensive" at 31 times run-rate sales but likes "the accelerated prospects." He added 2.1%. On November 1 he had kept it a tracker because of its profitability focus. [E38] [E39] [E11]

## AppLovin: re-initiated at 3%

Revenue of $2.185bn grew 56% above a $2.055-2.085bn guide; adjusted EBITDA margin reached 84%, guided to 84-85% in Q4. Q4 revenue guidance implies 44% growth, a deceleration, "but there was enough positivity elsewhere in the report to put me over the line." Free cash flow at 77% of adjusted EBITDA, up from 53%, was one deciding factor. [E40] [E41]

On why he did not simply keep his earlier shares: "In Q2, management said they didn't meet their standards and there was no way of knowing for sure if the company would actually reacelerate." He exited AppLovin after earnings disappointment in August. [E42]
