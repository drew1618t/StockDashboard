## Richardson Electronics: a small company with one strong quarter

WPR describes a distributor and manufacturer spanning power/microwave technology, semiconductor-fab equipment, energy products, and custom displays. The semiconductor-fab exposure interests him most. He reports quarterly revenue stepping up from a recent range of roughly $52m-$56m to $66m, with 28% annual growth versus low single-digit growth in several preceding quarters. Reported net income rises to $3.7m. [E03]

At a stated $250m market capitalization and 0.95× run-rate sales, he sees a business priced for little growth. He notes $31.8m cash and a data-provider debt figure of $1.4m, while management says there is no outstanding revolving-credit debt. He interprets effective debt as near zero, but the source does not reconcile every debt category. [E03]

His earnings-call review finds growth across all three segments, new products, expanding backlog, and an expected multimillion-dollar battery-energy-storage order in the next fiscal quarter. He discusses manufacturing and engineering expansion, display applications, and an internal AI project. Management also admits limited visibility in semiconductor-fab demand despite positive customer feedback. [E04] [E05] [E06]

The main risk is that a single strong quarter reflects project timing. Richardson provides no revenue guidance. WPR wants the next quarter above $70m, compared with his cited analyst estimate of $59m, and asks whether quarterly revenue could eventually exceed $100m. Those are his desired outcome and open question, not company guidance. He takes a starter while evaluating the durability of growth. [E07]

## Red Violet: profit growth and competitive wins outweigh modest revenue growth

WPR describes identity intelligence and investigative tools, including IDI and Forewarn, with US exposure across several industries. He reports revenue of $26.7m, up 23%, and acknowledges that this top-line rate is not especially strong for his approach. What attracts him is the improvement in earnings and operating leverage: $5m reported net income, 85.7% gross margin, $7.2m adjusted net income up 58%, $11.2m adjusted EBITDA up 48%, and $10.6m operating cash flow up 42%. These measures have different accounting bases and should remain distinct. [E08]

He recounts record customer additions, four of five verticals reaching record revenue, expansion of Forewarn into home healthcare, and a $109m public offering intended for working capital and possible acquisitions. He would prefer organic growth. Real-estate activity outside Forewarn is a concern, and the approximately 10.4× run-rate sales valuation already reflects market recognition. [E09] [E11]

The deciding evidence is a reported competitive win: management says a law-enforcement customer chose IDI even after the incumbent cut its price, because the customer preferred the product and investigative results. This is a customer anecdote reported by management and recounted by WPR, not an independently interviewed customer. WPR explicitly says displacement of larger incumbents pushed him into taking a starter. [E10] [E11]

His optimism exceeds what the 23% revenue growth alone would suggest. That does not establish an eventual acceleration; it records his willingness to combine improving economics and specific competitive evidence with a less impressive current revenue rate. [E11]

## Clear Secure: interested, but not yet bought

He treats Clear Secure as a different identity market from Red Violet, centered on airport access and verification. He reports $278m revenue, 27% annual growth, 69% gross margin, $50m net income, and a $5.9bn market cap at 5.3× run-rate sales. Cash generation and rising margins attract him; his cited free cash flow is $189m, up 60%, and adjusted EBITDA margin is 36.4%. Next-quarter revenue guidance is $284m-$287m. [E12]

He considers a small starter because growth and profitability are improving at a valuation he finds reasonable. His questions are how quickly the company can scale and whether airport coverage could approach saturation. The video does not disclose a purchase. The captioned ticker is unclear, so this report uses the company name without guessing it. [E13]

## Semtech: likely entry, with an explicit knowledge gap

WPR reports $342m quarterly revenue, above $328m guidance, with 33% annual and 17% sequential growth. Next-quarter guidance of $410m implies 54% annual growth against the 35% analysts had expected, and 20% sequential growth. He thinks a beat could take annual growth to about 60%; that is his forecast, not issued guidance. [E14]

Data-center demand and the photonics portfolio interest him. Most striking is management's plan to increase fab capacity three to four times by year-end. He explicitly says he does not know whether that translates into company-wide revenue or applies only to specific product lines. He is likely to start a position, while tempering enthusiasm relative to Astera, Credo, and Lumentum. No completed Semtech purchase is disclosed here. [E15]

This uncertainty matters to the chronology. In the September 6 video he reports having bought and added to Semtech, then explains that the fab serves a small photonics operation and considers reducing the position. The later clarification resolves a question he openly left unanswered here. It should not be rewritten as something he already knew on August 30. ## IREN: growth potential survives, but confidence falls

WPR calls the report difficult to prepare for and interpret. Bitcoin mining, miner decommissioning, AI-cloud revenue, ARR targets, financing, and GPU economics create too many moving parts for his usual revenue-estimate process. He says feeling nervous without an objective benchmark is a reason to investigate, rather than proof by itself that the business is weak. [E16]

His preparation includes a minimum $65m AI-cloud revenue target, compared with $33.6m previously, and a hope that the already announced $4bn year-end ARR target might rise above $4.1bn. He puts more weight on the forward ARR outlook than the current quarter but dislikes relying on a vague “strong narrative.” [E16]

Sources: [E18] [E19]. Amounts, periods, and accounting treatment are WPR's recounting. Capex relative to market capitalization describes his concern, not a financing-coverage ratio.

His objections include revenue appearing far down the release; ambiguity between quarter-end ARR and “today” ARR; inconsistent-looking cash, debt, and impairment figures; rapid hiring; and no separately listed R&D expense. He does not claim fraud and explicitly says the differing figures may have legitimate explanations. Absence of a separate R&D line is not proof that no research occurs. [E17] [E18] [E19]

He still likes vertical integration across data centers, compute, and software, and recognizes potential upside if the business executes. He notices cloud cost-of-revenue figures that look attractive, but this does not establish complete unit economics after depreciation, financing, overhead, and future capital needs. His principal worry is placing too much trust in a heavily back-loaded ramp while realized profits deteriorate. [E18] [E19] [E20]

His action is a substantial reduction, not an exit: the disclosed end-of-week holding is 2.6%. He may cut further and says he can rebuild after stronger evidence. Some original shares had reached a long-term capital-gains holding threshold, which influenced his willingness to wait through the report. He says tax considerations are usually not overriding. This is his account of his own tax timing, not tax guidance. [E01] [E02] [E20]

### Phison

## Comparison lessons from this episode

The following is this report's synthesis of the evidence, not a new set of rules attributed verbatim to WPR.

- Entry can occur before a long track record of acceleration. Richardson has one strong quarter; WPR uses a starter and identifies the next result he wants to see. [E07]
- Revenue growth alone does not rank the ideas. Red Violet's 23% growth attracts a starter because of margins, earnings, and competitive displacement; ADATA's much faster growth does not protect it from a sale when margins deteriorate. [E11] [E23]
- Research can begin with an unresolved question. Semtech's fab contribution is uncertain here and clarified later. Preserve the question alongside the initial enthusiasm. [E15]
- More operating scale is not enough if economics and funding remain difficult to evaluate. IREN's potential remains attractive to him while confidence and allocation fall. [E20]
- Source access is itself a research constraint. WPR accepts incomplete English materials for Innodisk, but explicitly acknowledges reliance on financials and AI-assisted understanding. [E21] [E23]
