# WPR's growth stocks and new portfolio additions

October 18, 2026 | Episode 7 | [Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wzsOkiMNb5E) | [Full transcript](transcript.md) | [Exact evidence](evidence.md)

WPR sold Micron and split the proceeds evenly into a new DigitalOcean position and a Penguin Solutions reentry. He also trimmed Astera Labs to start Clear Secure as a small step toward diversification outside AI. He chose DigitalOcean over Cloudflare, which stays on his watchlist. Boost Run, SOLV Energy and Frequency Electronics are passed ideas. The week's AI and semiconductor drawdown cut his self-reported year-to-date return from 31% to 11%. ## Market commentary and strategy

WPR attributes the week's drop to three negative stories: Chinese memory maker CXMT flooding the market with cheap memory, CoreWeave's bonds and financing blowing up, and banks saying hyperscalers could cut AI capex. His point is that all three remain hypotheticals. He says CXMT produces about a third of what each of the big three memory makers produces, new fabs take time, and its reported 87% gross margin gives it little reason to sell cheaply. Higher CoreWeave bond rates are not insolvency in his view, and hyperscalers have not yet cut capex. These are his interpretations and source-reported figures. [E01]

He contrasts the market with the 2000 internet bubble and the 2022 SaaS crash: demand for compute, memory and photonics remains strong, and many of his semiconductor stocks look cheap to him. Company financials have not deteriorated "at least not yet", and he expects earnings season to show which companies were "just talking a big game." [E01] [E02]

He calls the week stressful, says he probably should be more diversified, and took a small step toward that. He avoids leverage, suggests a large AI fund may have been forced to sell, and says his strategy avoids drastic moves during downturns. He plans optional membership posts for noteworthy weeks and a strategy video on mental game in down markets. [E02] [E23]

## Clear Secure: executed diversification starter

He had been reconsidering Clear Secure for a while and points to his [August 30 discussion](../2026-08-30_8qA2BK-Yt6Q/report.md), where it was a possible starter without a purchase. He cites a strong brand in airline identity, government regulatory buy-in, and a recent press release about a Veterans Affairs pilot that he sees as a significant TAM expansion if Clear moves beyond travel. He reports $278m revenue, up 27%, 68.6% gross margin, 31% EBITDA margin and 18% net income margin, calling all four records for the past year. He sees it as fully insulated from the AI buildout with no supply-chain issues. [E05]

The $278m revenue figure matches the August 30 source, so this appears to be the same quarter. August 30 cited a 36.4% adjusted EBITDA margin, while October 18 says 31% EBITDA margin. These are likely different measures and are not merged here. The ticker is not spoken; the October 18 slide lists Clear Secure as YOU. [E05]

## DigitalOcean: executed starter

The captions give the symbol as "D ON"; the October 18 slide lists DOCN. WPR describes a cloud platform moving up market from startups, developers and small businesses to enterprises and AI labs, with infrastructure, platform and some software services. Competitors include the hyperscalers, neoclouds and startups. [E06]

He reports quarterly revenue of $219m, $230m, $242m, $258m and $281m, with annual growth rising every quarter from 14% to 29%. The latest quarter beat the $272m-$274m guide by $7m. Next-quarter guidance tops out at $307m, or 34% growth, and implied Q4 guidance is $334m, or 38%. EBITDA rose from $66m to $77m and net income moved from $37m to $35m, which he flags as lagging revenue. Gross margin is 55%, the bottom of a 55%-60% range, which he attributes to onboarding larger enterprises. He cites $767m cash, $1.98bn debt, a $13.2bn market cap and a run-rate P/S of 11.7, and calls it fairly priced. All figures are source-reported. [E06]

In his recap of the CEO's remarks, inference services grew almost 800%, the April-launched inference engine has more than 6,000 customers, the company landed its first 9-figure annual commitment, and it added a record $93m of incremental ARR. AI customer ARR is $234m, growing over 200%, with 85% from inference and cloud rather than bare metal. Management expects about 30% growth for 2026 and is more confident in over 50% for 2027, without formal 2027 guidance. It reported raising GPU list prices about 30%. [E07]

The CFO, as recounted, said customers spending over $1m grew 214%, the top 25 customers are 20% of ARR, and record 102% NDR will no longer be disclosed. It equitized $472m of 2030 convertible notes and raised free-cash-flow margin guidance to 11%-13%. In Q&A, management said it adds more ARR per megawatt than neocloud peers because it sells higher-layer services to a broader customer base. It said upside from new capacity and deals is not reflected in the over-50% estimate. [E08] [E09]

WPR says headline numbers did not get his attention at first. The 2027 projection and the Q&A details about upside are the standout, along with GAAP profitability while scaling. Concerns are $1.98bn debt and gross margin at 55% versus as high as 60%. Since he bought, the stock has traded down with CoreWeave, but he sees a big difference: DigitalOcean is currently profitable and much less reliant on debt financing. [E10]

His valuation comments are inconsistent in the captions: the overview says a $13.2bn market cap, while the trade rationale says $12bn. Both are retained as spoken. [E04] [E06]

## Cloudflare: watchlist, not bought

WPR owned Cloudflare for about four years after its 2019 IPO, and one of his first earnings writeups covered its Q3 2020 report. He reports $696m revenue, up 36%, accelerating from 28% a year ago, a $31m beat, and next-quarter guidance of $737m, or 31%. EBITDA was $3m. Net income was -$170m, which he says would have been $18m without a one-time event. Gross margin is 72%, at the bottom of a 72%-75% range. He cites $4.16bn cash, $3.53bn debt, a $124bn market cap and run-rate P/S of 45. [E11]

His recap of prepared remarks includes 4,698 customers spending over $100,000, up 27%, 120% net retention, more than 80,000 paying customer additions, several multimillion-dollar contracts, more than half of network traffic being non-human, and a new monetization gateway. Management explained lower gross margin with network costs and moving sales and marketing items into cost of revenue. He finds that regrouping strange this late. [E12]

He criticizes Cloudflare for reporting adjusted operating and net income without the "adjusted" prefix. He cites GAAP operating margin of -29.6%, or -7.9% excluding the one-time event, and believes that is why management avoids GAAP metrics. He questions why Cloudflare does not charge anything for blocking malicious AI traffic, notes it is open-sourcing an internal operating system, and asks why a goal of GAAP profitability by the end of 2028 is not moved up if the company is in "striking distance." [E13] [E14]

His Q3 2020 retrospective cites $114m revenue, 54% growth and 77% gross margin. He judges Cloudflare at a $5bn-$10bn market cap then as a more compelling opportunity than today, while the long-term opportunity remains strong. [E15]

His conclusion: reaccelerating growth, customer growth across all tiers and a popular agentic AI platform make a very compelling narrative. His concern is that profitability "seems like an afterthought." He grants that shareholders have been rewarded without profits and compares it to the SaaS-era idea of becoming ubiquitous before monetizing. [E16]

| Latest revenue and growth | $281m, 29% | $696m, 36% |
| Next-quarter guided growth | 34% | 31% |
| EBITDA | $77m | $3m |
| Net income | $35m | -$170m ($18m excluding one-time cost) |
| Gross margin | 55% | 72% |
| Adjusted operating margin | 24% | 13.8% |
| Employees | about 1,460 | about 5,200 |
| Run-rate P/S | 11.7 | 45 |
| Cash and debt | $767m / $1.98bn | $4.16bn / $3.53bn |
| Long-term indication | over 50% growth in 2027 | GAAP profitable by end of 2028 |

He sees growth rates as comparable, DigitalOcean as far more profitable and cheaper, and Cloudflare as stronger on gross margin and balance sheet. He attributes Cloudflare's lower operating margin despite higher gross margin to headcount and San Francisco salaries; that is his interpretation. Both companies sit at the bottom of their gross-margin ranges, which he thinks may reflect larger customers negotiating harder. [E17]

The decisive factor was long-term management indications: he found DigitalOcean's 2027 growth commentary much more impressive than Cloudflare's distant profitability target. He would consider Cloudflare if management mentioned raising prices or the stock price came down. [E18]

## Passed ideas

### Boost Run

Boost Run (BRUN) is a neocloud that went public via SPAC in May; he began researching it before neocloud stocks fell. He reports $31.1m revenue versus $11m last quarter and $4.1m a year ago, analyst estimates rising to $136m within four quarters, -$75m net income possibly from one-time or IPO costs, $120m cash, $436m debt, a $710m market cap, 94% gross margin and 5.7x run-rate sales. Management claims include $1.9bn of contracted value, 22% average prepayment, a $400m year-end ARR target and a $1.44bn Dell purchase agreement that is "essentially fully committed"; the word "essentially" did not inspire confidence. [E19]

His large concern is headcount: Robinhood shows three employees, and AI tools told him LinkedIn shows 11-50. That AI-sourced headcount is unverified. He says it is worth following, and GAAP profitability while scaling revenue could change his mind. [E19]

### SOLV Energy

The captions give the symbol as "MW"; SOLV Energy trades as MWH. It is an engineering, procurement and construction contractor for solar, storage and electrical infrastructure, which IPO'd in February 2026. He reports $951m revenue, up 77%, 14.8% gross margin at the bottom of its range, $364m cash, $84m debt, a $4.9bn market cap and 1.3x run-rate sales, which he calls fair for a low-margin business. He likes that the reported $8.9bn backlog is almost double the market cap. American Securities' reported 80% ownership is "debatably one red flag." Private-equity control and low margins give him pause. [E20]

### Frequency Electronics

The captions give the symbol as "FEM"; Frequency Electronics trades as FEIM. It makes precision timing and RF hardware for government, defense and satellite markets. He reports $23.5m revenue, up 70% annually and 35% sequentially, 46% gross margin after a big step up, a $780m market cap, 8.3x run-rate sales and a net cash position. Management-reported backlog reached a record $129m, up 82%. A customer reportedly wants monthly production increases of 50%. [E21]

The company gives no guidance, so he cannot tell whether the step up is one-time. Analysts project $18.4m next quarter, a significant sequential decline, and management targets $150m annual revenue by fiscal 2029. His take is that the business sounds like it is growing much faster than analysts, and possibly management, expect. This is an opinion on a passed idea, not a forecast verified here. [E21]

## Plan for earnings season

He is likely to hold off on significant changes before earnings season. A disappointing report would free up cash for more promising names, and he plans to invest behind exceptional results. This is a stated plan, not an executed action. Episode 8 is scheduled for October 25 and an earnings calendar update for October 29, as stated on October 18. [E22] [E23]
