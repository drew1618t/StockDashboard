## MKS: acceleration at a lower sales multiple, with debt

WPR sees MKS as broad exposure to semiconductor investment through power and control systems, instruments, chemistry, photonics and other equipment. He highlights NAND upgrades and new factories, advanced packaging, and expanding chemistry capacity. These business descriptions and management outlooks are his account of the company. [E07] [E09] [E11]

He reports $1.25 billion quarterly revenue, up 28% annually and 10% sequentially, versus $973 million a year earlier. The next-quarter revenue guide is $1.35 billion, implying 37% annual growth in his calculation. His expectation that another beat could take growth above 40% is distinct from company guidance. He cites $175 million net income versus $62 million a year earlier and $84 million in the prior quarter, alongside approximately 48% gross margin. [E08]

The earnings-call review supplies a more specific catalyst: management expects semiconductor revenue growth above 50% next quarter and describes visibility extending through 2027. WPR recounts $188 million free cash flow and a $100 million debt prepayment. The operating-margin guide he quotes is 26.3%; a result above 27% is his own expectation. [E09] [E10]

The objection is $4.19 billion of debt against $611 million cash, attributed to a 2022 acquisition. He calls this a yellow flag but accepts it given repayment plans. At a stated $15.7 billion market cap and 3.1 times annualized quarterly sales, he considers valuation attractive. That sales multiple excludes debt and should not be mistaken for an enterprise-value multiple. His action is a starter position while he learns more. [E08] [E12]

## FormFactor: testing growth with improving economics

FormFactor supplies wafer probe cards and test systems. WPR is attracted to exposure across high-bandwidth memory, co-packaged optics, and custom hyperscaler ASICs. He describes an established customer base including SK hynix, TSMC, Intel and Samsung, with emerging opportunities as more demanding chips require advanced probes. [E13] [E17] [E18]

He reports $258 million quarterly revenue, up 32% annually and 14% sequentially, exceeding the $245 million guidance ceiling by $13 million. Net income is about $56 million, versus $9 million a year earlier and $20 million in the prior quarter. Next-quarter guidance is $270 million plus or minus $10 million. WPR considers the midpoint's roughly 5% sequential growth more acceptable after considering seasonality. [E14] [E16]

He cites $346 million cash, $20 million debt, an $8 billion market cap and 7.8 times annualized quarterly sales. His comparison with Technoprobe makes FormFactor look reasonably priced to him: he describes similar quarterly revenue at a competitor valued above $20 billion. This is WPR's comparison, not an independently normalized valuation across companies, currencies or accounting bases. [E14] [E18]

Management, in his account, expects the Texas facility to come online in Q4 and ramp through 2027 at a lower cost structure. He recounts $52.6 million free cash flow and largely internally funded expansion. He also reports an increased 2026 CPO revenue expectation above $20 million, with further growth discussed for Q4. Those revenue comments are distinct from WPR's inference that prototype testing explains early CPO sales. [E15] [E16] [E17] [E18]

Two questions remain. First, the stated goal of doubling revenue by 2030 looks slow to him; he wonders whether it is an older target carried forward. Second, he notices a discrepancy between the CFO's 50.7% GAAP gross margin and his data provider's 52.4%. The report preserves that disagreement. It also treats possible Nvidia CPO involvement and an analyst's third-hyperscaler ramp comment as qualified evidence, not confirmed customer-volume disclosures. He plans further investor-day and conference research after buying. [E15] [E16] [E17] [E18]

## Coherent: an earnings watch, with a possible future starter

WPR's interest changes as revenue growth accelerates from the repeated 17% annual growth he recalls. He reports $2.05 billion quarterly revenue, up 34% annually and 13% sequentially, exactly at the prior guidance ceiling. The next guide is $2.2 billion to $2.4 billion; his 52% annual-growth calculation uses the top end, not the midpoint. He cites a $60 billion market cap and 7.3 times annualized quarterly sales, which he considers reasonable. [E20] [E24]

Management's 42% pro-forma annual growth figure excludes the sold defense business; it is not the same measure as reported 34% growth. WPR recounts management expecting a quarter above $3 billion revenue by fiscal 2027 year-end, supported by demand, capacity and new product streams. Optical circuit switches, CPO, next-generation transceivers and Photon Link are the main opportunities he discusses. [E19] [E21] [E22]

Margins restrain his enthusiasm. He quotes 40.2% adjusted gross margin and 21.8% adjusted operating margin, both improving, but lower than he expects for the company's scale. He notes industrial weakness while management expects new technologies to support future margins. [E23]

His executed research action is adding Coherent to the earnings calendar. He may buy a starter on earnings day because he expects a strong result; he does not disclose buying it in this video. Learning about Coherent also changes how he assesses Lumentum's competition and contributes to that trim. [E24] [E05]

## Two passes

Optical Cable (OCC) reports, in WPR's account, $24.3 million revenue, 22% annual growth, and gross margin of 37.4%, versus 31.7% a year earlier. He wants revenue to exceed the roughly $25 million level reached in 2018 with expanding margins. No forward guidance, no analyst coverage, limited end-customer visibility, and partnership answers he considers vague hold him back. His concern that management may be content to remain small is an interpretation of its behavior. The partnership name is garbled in captions and left unresolved here. [E25]

Kennametal (KMT) appears unusually cheap to him at a stated $2.3 billion market cap and 0.75 times annualized sales. He recounts $737 million quarterly revenue, 43% annual growth, and large profitability gains. But he cannot explain confidently how much comes from favorable tungsten pricing or the company's resource arrangements. He passes and adds it to the earnings calendar. His speculation that the company might deserve roughly $7 billion market value is not a valuation established by this report. [E26] [E27]
