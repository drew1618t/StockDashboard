# Silicon Motion: margins far ahead, a softer revenue guide, and a plan to stay high

October 29, 2026 · *Growth Stock SIMO shows continued growth in Q3 2026* · Earnings reported October 28

[Source video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5zGsnheZAkM) · [Formatted transcript](transcript.md) · [Exact evidence](evidence.md) · [Structured company view](company_views.json)

WPR says he was "impressed with where the business is heading" and is "expecting to maintain a high allocation position in Silicon Motion." That is a stated plan. He discloses no trade and no new weight in this video. [E15]

Q3 revenue landed exactly on his $560m hurdle and above the company's guidance range. The Q4 revenue guide of $616m at the top end missed his $630m hurdle, but he says margins "more than made up for it": the 37% adjusted operating margin guide was far above the 30% he wanted. [E04] [E05] [E06]

All financial figures and management statements here are WPR's account of the press release and earnings call. They have not been checked against company filings or audio. Later price performance is not used to judge this view.

Q3 revenue was about 3.5% above the top of the guide. The guide's growth rates and the result's growth rates imply the same base periods within rounding. That arithmetic is this report's. [E03] [E05]

The prior Q2 release, as he quotes it, spoke of a strong backlog and strong customer forecasts and said the company expected "continued strong topline growth through the rest of the year." [E03]

He finds it interesting that gross margin, which seemed likely to top out near 50%, keeps rising, and says the business "is finding some efficiencies somehow." [E06]

WPR says the boot drives "barely had any sales last year" and now make up a significant portion of the business. In Q&A, management put Ferri and boot drive at 40% of sales, rising to 50% next year. [E08] [E12]

He ties the eMMC and UFS decline to consumer demand, citing the release's statement that rising memory prices create pricing pressure in consumer. The release also says enterprise and Ferri customers prioritize supply assurance over price, expects continued momentum in Q4, and calls enterprise and AI products early in their revenue contribution. ## What management said, as WPR recounts it

**Margins and competition.** The CEO attributes gross margin gains to auto, AI and enterprise storage. NAND makers are outsourcing controllers more, and the company wins on performance, power and cost. [E09]

**Enterprise.** MonTitan, captioned "Mont Titan," is winning across NAND makers, hyperscalers and cloud providers, with a revenue ramp expected on next-generation PCIe 6 controllers and design wins in networking and AI. It ramps over the next two years with improved visibility. [E09] [E10]

**PC share.** PCIe 5 solutions are 10% of the PC market, expected to reach 20-25% by the end of 2027 and 40% by 2028, excluding Apple. [E10]

**Consumer.** PC and smartphone markets are struggling with memory price increases, and some consumer products are being de-specced. The CEO expects consumer to return to growth next year. Management expects NAND prices to stay relatively flat for the next two quarters. [E09] [E10] [E12]

**Auto and robotics.** Auto backlog rose significantly, the company works with nearly every automaker, and certifications and firmware support long-term auto growth. It is engaged with several humanoid customers and says robotics may become larger than auto. [E09] [E10] [E12]

**Boot drives.** Silicon Motion is the primary supplier of DRAM-less boot drives and is now producing boot drives with DRAM. The Ferri and boot-drive backlog increased significantly in the last three months. Boot-drive customers have diversified, and the segment is expected to grow "even much faster next year." DRAM boot drives are ready in Q1 2027, with a second-half ramp. [E10] [E12]

**Balance sheet and costs.** The CFO cites a $1.13bn capital raise in August and $954m cash. Adjusted operating expense fell to $120.7m from $122.1m, which surprised WPR given rising revenue. The captions give no reason why cash is below the raise amount. [E11]

**2027.** The CFO said revenue can grow by at least 40% next year, with more definitive guidance in Q4. WPR thinks that is "a little bit of a low estimate" and notes that this management team has typically guided conservatively. Management also expects operating margin leverage next year and describes longer product cycles, higher barriers to entry, less consumer-cycle impact, and more predictable revenue. [E11] [E12]

**Supply and China.** Management describes a strong supply chain on test, assembly and substrate, agreements with TSMC, and says the August raise sets the company up for the next couple of years. Asked about China, management sees no competitive threat on memory controllers. [E12]

## Analyst estimates

WPR shows analyst projections before they had updated for the report. They had Q4 revenue at $550m and Q4 2028 revenue at $668m. He reads this as "practically no growth" and says analysts are "still underestimating Silicon Motion." [E13]

Analysts project Q4 2028 adjusted EPS of $4.74 against $4.35 delivered this quarter. He says this conflicts with management's statements about operating leverage through 2027. [E14]

For scale: $668m is about 8% above the new $616m Q4 guide top end, and $4.74 is about 9% above this quarter's adjusted EPS. That arithmetic is this report's, not WPR's.

## Conclusion and action

WPR's conclusions, in his own framing: [E15]

- Enterprise solutions are gaining massive traction and are more durable and consistent than consumer.
- Near-term consumer weakness explains the 10% sequential Q4 guide, which is still strong annual growth.
- Profitability and margins were the big standouts.
- He expects to maintain a high allocation position.
