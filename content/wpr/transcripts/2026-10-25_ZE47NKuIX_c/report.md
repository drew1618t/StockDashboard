## Why he sold Forgent and BESI

Forgent CFO departure on October 22, described as for family reasons, compounded an existing concern about Neos Partners owning 37% and potentially selling more. WPR questions whether he would buy the stock if discovering it today. He initially retained a small position because it was cheap and he was still thinking, then sold the remainder after considering the CFO short tenure and sponsor uncertainty. The stated $7.12bn cumulative sponsor share sales are source-reported. He could revisit if the sponsor sells more and earnings continue to impress. That is conditional interest after an exit. [E03] [E05]

BESI reported, in his account, $321m revenue, +114% annually and +13% sequentially, but guided only +5% sequential growth to an implied $337m. He contrasts a prior $214m to $285m jump with the subsequent deceleration. Gross margin was 65.2%, and the Q4 range was 63%-65%. A CEO comment dismissing a customer despec as noise further reduced his confidence. An analyst suggested order pushouts; that explanation is the analyst view. WPR wanted outstanding results from this international holding and says the report gave him a clear sell decision. [E04]

## Clear Secure, DigitalOcean, Ethos and Cloudflare

Clear Secure received two additions totaling 1.0% this week. He remains enthusiastic about the business and diversification, but flags a viewer comment that bookings growth guidance is about 20%, lower than earlier quarters. He will reevaluate at earnings. This is a source-reported watchpoint, not independently checked guidance. DigitalOcean received 0.2%; he again highlights its indicated 2027 revenue acceleration. [E03]

Ethos remained above 18% after its price strength. He is confident about the business and upcoming report, but the oversized allocation did not match his enthusiasm. The 0.8% trim was sizing rather than a negative earnings call. [E06]

Cloudflare moved from last week watchlist status to an executed 0.2% tracker. He cites AI and security tailwinds, Workers and trading behavior that seems different from AI hardware. He still worries the stock is overpriced and management is insufficiently focused on profitability. He plans to reevaluate both NET and YOU on their reports. This purchase does not erase the October 18 concerns or prove either earlier conditional buy trigger was met. [E06]

## Bloom: reentry after a strong report

WPR describes fuel-cell power generation with rapid delivery, low pollution and noise, and little direct competition of similar scale. His recounting includes Nebius adopting Bloom for its flagship site, Home Depot deployments and geographic expansion. Product advantages and customer assertions here are WPR descriptions and management claims, not a separately validated competitive study. [E08]

His financial recap gives Q3 revenue of $1.235bn, +138% annually, versus $1.07bn expected. Full-year guidance rises to $4.4bn-$4.6bn from $3.9bn-$4.2bn. He calculates top-end implied Q4 revenue of $1.549bn versus $1.21bn expected, a $339m gap. Gross margin is 33.75%, operating income $225m at an 18.2% margin, and net income $250m. All are source-reported; the report does not certify the accounting bases. [E09]

He prefers net income and EBITDA to free cash flow when expansion absorbs cash. He discusses receivables, inventory and property investment, and uses AI to interpret contract assets. His explanation of contract assets as not yet countable revenue or an intermediate backlog stage is retained as his understanding, not an accounting definition. His inventory interpretation also must not be read as a verified balance-sheet stock from a cash-flow change. [E10]

Management, as recounted, says backlog is growing faster than revenue and describes rapid Oracle and Nebius deployments. The CFO passage cites $286m operating income, separately from the earlier $225m, plus $299m adjusted EBITDA, $27m operating cash flow and negative $181m quarterly free cash flow. WPR dislikes calling negative cash flow a “use” and citing $2.5bn cash without $2.78bn debt. He also criticizes lengthy backlog discussion without the backlog number. The two operating-income figures may use different bases; the captions do not establish that reconciliation. Likewise, $27m operating cash flow less the earlier $153m property purchases does not directly reconcile to negative $181m FCF. No reconciliation is invented. [E11] [E12]

The Q&A includes management aspirations for cost reductions and a self-funding business; these are not achieved future outcomes. WPR is pleased to reenter after the share-price decline, but retains scandium supply and CFO disclosure concerns. He is considering increasing the position after other holdings report. Only the 0.2% purchase is executed in this source. His momentum-trader explanation for the selloff is an interpretation, not verified causation. [E13] [E14]

## Earnings screening and Vicor watchlist

His calendar sent three non-holdings through a first-pass earnings screen. NVE revenue of $5.83m fell far below his above-$13m hurdle after a prior $11m quarter; his pull-forward explanation is an inference. MaxLinear posted $218m against $215m expected and guided $240m-$260m against $227m expected, but its posted metrics did not stand out enough for more investigation. Bloom cleared the screen and received deeper research. [E07]

Vicor (VICR) makes power-conversion modules for demanding computing systems. He identifies Monolithic Power as competition and discusses Cerebras as a major customer; current Nvidia exposure is explicitly uncertain. His overview reports Q2 revenue of $143m and Q3 revenue of $197m, +79% annually and +38% sequentially, with Q3 gross margin 68.3%, net income $63m and backlog $406m. Cash is $513m versus $8m debt. [E15]

The stated $9.3bn market cap and 16.2x run-rate sales use a basis close to the older $143m quarter. Using the stated Q3 revenue would give roughly 11.8x. That is an editorial arithmetic observation, not WPR changing his valuation view. A later backlog caption says “46 million,” inconsistent with the earlier $406m. Neither is silently corrected. [E15] [E17]

Management long-term aspirations are $2.5bn annual revenue and 70% gross margin with no timeline. New fabs promise much more capacity, but WPR notes they take time. Q3 royalty revenue of $67m includes a nonrecurring sale; a Q4 floor of $43m and informal product-growth guidance of about 10% sequentially do not impress him as much as the just-reported quarter. Management later calls the royalty floor conservative and excluding new deals. He also watches the operating-expense jump and discounts potential patent recoveries. [E16] [E17] [E18]

He likes exposure to next-generation computing and the planned capacity increase, but near-term constraints and informal guidance prevent a purchase. He adds Vicor to the Q4 earnings calendar to see where the next quarter lands. [E19]

## Passed ideas

Pure Cycle is the Colorado water and land-development business, PCYO, captioned “PYCO.” He reports $8.2m quarterly revenue, +60%, $2.9m net income, 52% gross margin, $8m cash and $14m debt. A $263m market cap gives about 8x run-rate sales. He flags thin trading, roughly $250,000 a day, and difficulty valuing a small growing water utility. Management describes recurring customers, industrial demand and previously acquired land/water assets. WPR remains uncertain about scaling and expansion outside Colorado; he wants revenue closer to $20m. [E20]

PicS N.V. operates PicPay in Brazil and trades as PICS, garbled as “Pix” and “P IC.” WPR reports $797m revenue, +67%, $52m net income, $1.85bn market cap and 0.58x run-rate sales. He contrasts that sales multiple with Nubank at 7.8x. Management-call figures of R$1.25bn gross profit and R$283m adjusted net income use Brazilian currency and are kept separate from his earlier converted figures. Accounts, credit mix, improved nonperforming loans and an insurance acquisition support the growth narrative. [E21]

His decisive concern is the stated 96% voting control by a family associated with a past corruption scandal; the CFO transition is another risk he mentions. He nevertheless thinks fair value could exceed $5bn even allowing for those drawbacks. This is his valuation opinion on an explicitly passed idea, not an investment or an independently established fair value. [E21]

## Figure: keep sizing until earnings

He will judge Figure on revenue, profitability, revenue guidance and partner additions. He reports trailing P/E near 25 and believes the recent share decline already prices in bad news. The jump in blockchain loan assets might reflect Kiavi onboarding, but he explicitly frames that connection as his belief. [E22]

Weekly consumer-loan volume declined from $419m to $415m, $390m, $366m and $349m through week 42, October 12-18. The latest week was still +73% annually versus +95% in week 38. WPR is uncertain how much is seasonality; he also wonders about rates and political uncertainty. Monthly captions say “92 million” through October 18 but then a $1.55bn full-month run rate versus September at $1.78bn. The figures are internally inconsistent as captioned, so no corrected October total is asserted. [E23]

Democratized Prime Q3 matched offers, lender supply and borrower demand expanded from Q2. The captioned YLDS figures fell sharply; his idea that faster loan onboarding needs less token circulation is expressly a theory he is unsure of. He sees nothing too alarming overall and plans the same sizing until the November 12 earnings report, when management commentary can resolve uncertainty. The earnings date and 8:30 a.m. timing are his stated schedule, not independently verified. [E24] [E25]
